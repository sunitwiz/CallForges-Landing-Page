What is an auto dialer and how does it work?

An auto dialer is software that calls the phone numbers on your list for you, so your reps spend their time talking instead of dialing. When a call ends, the rep logs the outcome and the dialer calls the next lead.

How does an auto dialer work?

Every auto dialer starts with a list: a set of leads with phone numbers, usually from your CRM or a spreadsheet. You load the list into a campaign, choose a few settings, and start a session. From there the dialer handles the mechanics:

  1. It picks the next lead on the list and places the call from your business number.
  2. It works out what happened: an answer, a voicemail box, a busy line or no answer.
  3. When a lead answers, it connects them to the rep and shows the lead's record.
  4. When the call ends, the rep logs the outcome and the dialer moves on.

Modern auto dialers run in the browser. The rep wears a headset, the calls go over the internet, and there is no desk phone or extra hardware. The dialer also keeps a record of every call, so managers can see who was called, what happened and what comes next.

In CallForges, a rep opens a campaign and clicks Start Campaign. The dialer calls the first lead, and the lead's contact page opens when they answer. After the call, a short countdown runs before the next lead rings. The rep can skip it, extend it, or log the outcome first.

What are the types of auto dialer?

Auto dialers differ in one main way: how many calls they place at once, and who decides. Here are the four types you will see most often:

TypeHow it dialsRep on the line when a lead answers?Best for
Preview dialerShows the rep the next lead, and the rep clicks to callYesSmall lists of high-value accounts
Power dialerOne call at a time, the next one starts when the last endsYesMost sales teams, warm leads, consumer lists
Parallel dialerSeveral leads at once for one rep, the first answer is connectedYes, for the first answerLarge business lists where most calls go unanswered
Predictive dialerSeveral calls per agent, timed by an algorithmOnly if an agent is freeLarge call centers with many agents

You may also hear "progressive dialer". Most vendors use it for the same thing as a power dialer: one call per rep, placed when the rep is free.

A power dialer is the safe default. It removes the dialing and keeps the rep on every call. A parallel dialer gets a single rep through a big cold list faster. A predictive dialer needs a large team to work well, which we explain in power dialer vs predictive dialer.

What else does an auto dialer do?

Dialing is the core, but the features around it decide how much time a team really saves. These are the ones worth checking, with how CallForges handles each:

  • Voicemail drop. Record a message once and play it into a lead's voicemail box with one click. In CallForges you pick a message and click Drop voicemail, and the campaign moves on while it plays.
  • Calling windows. Only call leads during their own working hours. CallForges uses each lead's local time zone, with Active Days, a Start Time and an End Time you set per campaign.
  • Do Not Call lists. Skip anyone who asked not to be called. With Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists on, those leads show Do not call and are never dialed. Reps can add a lead to the list from the post-call form.
  • Call outcomes. A post-call form with a Disposition, a note and a rating, so every call is logged the same way. Managers can make the disposition required.
  • Call recording. Record every connected call in a campaign for coaching and review.
  • CRM logging. Send each call to the right record in your CRM, so reps never copy notes between tabs. CallForges logs calls to HubSpot, Pipedrive, Attio, HighLevel and Clio.
  • Follow-up campaigns. Call back everyone who didn't answer. In CallForges you tick those leads in a campaign, click Create campaign, and a follow-up opens with the same settings.
  • Local numbers. Call from a number in the lead's own country, so they see a local caller ID. CallForges has local numbers in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, and in 56 countries and territories in all.

Who needs an auto dialer?

Any team that makes more than a handful of outbound calls a day. The common ones:

  • SDR and inside sales teams working through prospect lists every day.
  • Agencies running outbound for their clients.
  • Founders who block an hour or two for their own prospecting.
  • Real estate and insurance agents calling leads, past clients and renewals.
  • Customer success teams calling accounts about renewals and onboarding.

The test is simple. If your reps spend more time looking up and typing numbers than talking, a dialer will give them that time back. If they make five calls a day to carefully researched accounts, dialing by hand from the CRM is fine.

Is an auto dialer legal?

Yes, using an auto dialer is legal. The rules are about how you use it: who you call, whether you have consent where it is needed, when you call, and what happens when a person answers.

In the US, the TCPA restricts calls made with an "automatic telephone dialing system". In Facebook v. Duguid (2021), the Supreme Court held that this term covers equipment that can store or produce numbers using a random or sequential number generator. Separate FCC rules in 47 CFR 64.1200 cover prerecorded messages, abandoned calls, calling hours and the do-not-call registry. The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule adds rules for telemarketing to consumers, and most business-to-business calls are exempt from it.

In the UK, the ICO's guidance on live marketing calls covers the Telephone Preference Service and consent, and Ofcom's rules cover silent and abandoned calls.

The dialer type matters for abandoned calls. A power dialer keeps the rep on the line for every call it places. Dialers that ring several leads at once can create calls that are answered and then dropped, which the abandoned-call rules target.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules depend on who you call, what you sell and where. Consent, calling hours and do-not-call rules are your responsibility under the CallForges Terms.
