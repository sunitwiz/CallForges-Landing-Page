What is a power dialer?

Monday morning, and a rep has 80 trial sign-ups to call. By hand, each call starts with finding the row and typing the number, and ends with typing the notes. A power dialer is an auto dialer that does the dialing for you. It calls your leads one at a time, and when you hang up and log the call, the next lead rings. You never type a number, and you're on the line for everyone who answers.

CallForges team · 18 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • A power dialer rings one lead at a time, so nobody picks up to an empty line.
  • It suits lists where people answer, such as inbound leads, callbacks and renewals. On a cold list where most calls ring out, a parallel dialer gets through faster.
  • The pause between calls is the setting to get right: long enough to log the call, short enough to keep the pace.

How does a power dialer work?

You load a list, start a session, and the dialer works down it from the top:

  1. It calls the next lead, with you already on the line.
  2. If they answer, you're talking, with their contact record open.
  3. When the call ends, you log it and a short countdown starts.
  4. When the countdown runs out, it calls the next lead.

Because it rings one lead at a time, nobody answers to silence while you're on another call. A parallel dialer rings several leads at once, which we come back to below.

As an example, say finding and typing each number takes 30 seconds. Across 80 leads that's 40 minutes of typing, and a power dialer hands those minutes back to conversations.

The pause between calls

The setting reps notice most is the pause after each call. Too short, and they start the next call with half a note typed. Too long, and reps drift into a coffee break.

A good power dialer lets the manager set a default and lets each rep bend it: skip the countdown, add a few seconds, or open the call log, which holds the next call until they save. We'd start with a short pause and let reps add time. You won't notice a long default from one call to the next, but over a two-hour block it costs you dials.

The call log is where each call gets an outcome and a note, and where a lead who asks not to be called goes on your do-not-call list. You can make the outcome required, so no call goes unlogged. Reps grumble about that rule, yet next week's follow-up list comes from those outcomes.

Voicemail drop on a power dialer

On a cold list, a lot of calls end at a voicemail greeting, and by the tenth time you've left the same message by hand, you sound like it. With voicemail drop you record the message once. At the greeting, you pick your recording and the dialer plays it into the voicemail box. Your side of the call ends there, and you're on to the next lead.

Keep two recordings, one for a first touch and one for a follow-up. Our power dialer vs parallel dialer guide shows how voicemail works on each.

Power dialer vs parallel and predictive dialers

The names get mixed up, so here are the common kinds side by side.

Manual dialingPower dialerParallel dialerPredictive dialer
Who dialsThe rep, by handThe dialerThe dialerThe dialer
Leads ringing at once, per repOneOneSeveralSeveral, set by an algorithm
Is the rep on the line when a lead answers?YesYesThe first lead to answer is connectedOnly if an agent is free
Best forA few high-value callsWarm leads, follow-ups, consumer lists, named accountsLarge business lists where most calls go unansweredLarge call centers with many agents

A parallel dialer rings several leads together and connects you to the first one who answers, which suits big cold business lists. A predictive dialer guesses how many lines to ring from how many agents will be free, so it needs a large team (see power dialer vs predictive dialer).

Our pick: when the people on a list tend to answer, use a power dialer. Inbound leads, booked callbacks and renewals pick up often, and each of those answers should reach a rep. We'd start there with consumer lists in the US and the UK too, since one call at a time with a rep on the line doesn't create the dropped calls the rules target.

The annoying part is a cold list. Say nine in ten calls ring out: a power dialer still makes you sit through every ring. That list goes on a parallel dialer, and the power dialer keeps the leads who respond.

Is a power dialer legal?

Yes, in the US and the UK. The law looks at who you call and when, whether you may call them, and what happens when they pick up. The dialer type matters for that last part.

In the US, two federal rules, both law, count a call as abandoned when the person who answers isn't connected to a sales rep within two seconds of their greeting: the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule (16 CFR 310.4, as amended in 2024) and the FCC's 47 CFR 64.1200 (as amended in 2025). They target dialers that ring more people than there are reps to talk to. A power dialer places one call at a time with the rep on the line, so the rep is there when the lead answers.

Consent, do-not-call registers and calling hours apply however you dial, and so do the rules on recorded messages. In the US, the FTC's guide to the Telemarketing Sales Rule is a good place to start. In the UK, the rules on live marketing calls are law, and the ICO's guidance on live marketing calls explains them, including the Telephone Preference Service and consent.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules depend on who you call, what you sell and where. Consent, calling hours and do-not-call rules are your responsibility under the CallForges Terms.

Set up a power dialer in CallForges

Campaigns start with a paid plan, and your role needs Create and edit campaigns; owners, admins and members have it.

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign. Click the name at the top to rename it.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Power Dialer.
  3. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see, and set the Cooldown duration between two calls.
  4. Under Audience, click Select a list, or Upload leads for a CSV file with a phone number column.
  5. Turn on Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists and Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours, and set your calling hours.
  6. Choose whether to record calls and click Create Campaign. To change the order, click Edit and drag rows; leads are dialed top to bottom.
  7. Allow the browser to use your microphone and click Start Campaign.

The strip shows Power Dialing…, then Connected when the lead picks up. After the call, Wrapping up… shows the countdown. Click it (Skip Cooldown) to dial now, or +10s for more time. Log outcome opens the post-call form and holds the next call until you're done. There you pick a Disposition, add a Note and a Rating, and tick Add to Do Not Call list if the lead asked. An owner or admin can make the disposition required in Settings → Dialer settings → Call experience. To start each call by hand, turn Auto-advance off in the campaign's settings and click Dial next.

Record your voicemail messages in Settings → My voicemail → Voicemail drops, then click Drop voicemail when you reach a greeting.

Start during your leads' hours: a lead whose turn comes outside them shows Out of timezone and isn't called again in that campaign. Pause lets the current call finish and dials nothing new, and Resume picks up at the next lead. To call back everyone who didn't answer, tick those leads and click Create campaign for a follow-up with the same settings. The full steps are in Create a campaign and Start, pause, resume or stop a campaign.
