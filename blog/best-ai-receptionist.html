What makes a good AI receptionist for a small business

We judged each option on six things.

  • The billing model: per call, per minute, per unique caller, or a plan with a minute allowance. Per call suits long intake calls; per minute suits quick questions about hours. Our Smith.ai alternatives post runs the numbers for a 300-call month.
  • The question it can't answer. A good one admits it and offers a callback or a person; a bad one guesses and sounds certain.
  • The way to a person. Ask who it can transfer to, and whether that costs extra. A warm transfer, where your colleague hears who's calling first, beats a blind one.
  • Languages. Two of the tools below list English and Spanish only.
  • Hours. Check that you can choose when it answers. Most small teams start with nights and weekends, and holidays should follow the night path.
  • Disclosure. We'd only buy one that says it's an automated assistant up front and puts callers through to a person when they ask (What is an AI receptionist? explains why).

Best AI receptionist by business type

The right pick depends on who calls you and what they need when nobody's in.

Law firms

A first-time caller to a law firm usually wants a person. If someone must screen every new caller, Smith.ai's New Client Priority preset sends new-client calls to its receptionists, from $2.50 a call (Smith.ai Hybrid pricing), and even its free AI plan lists a Clio connection (Smith.ai AI pricing). We'd pick CallForges when night calls are office questions, such as what a consultation costs. Give the agent those answers and nothing that reads like legal advice. On Business, your calls are logged in Clio Manage (Clio integration).

Real estate agents

An agent at a showing can't take a buyer's call. If the AI should put viewings in your calendar, Rosie does it from its $149 plan (Rosie pricing) and Goodcall books into Google Calendar (Goodcall pricing); CallForges books a callback. CallForges pulls ahead once you also call old leads, since the power and parallel dialers come with every plan; a solo agent making 40 calls a day needs only the power dialer.

Home services

Plumbers and HVAC companies get two kinds of night calls: a leak that can't wait and a question that can. We'd send the leak to the person on call and let an AI take the rest. Goodcall's per-caller price suits customers who ring three times about one job, and Rosie suits a crew with Spanish-speaking customers. In CallForges, a call workflow can forward emergencies to the on-call mobile, and the agent can take your other after-hours calls.

Agencies

An insurance, marketing or staffing agency has several people on the phones, and callers want the right one. CallForges can ring a team in order by day and send nights to the agent, and on Business your calls are logged in HubSpot, Pipedrive, Attio or HighLevel. Agencies building receptionists for clients should look at Retell AI, and one already on RingCentral can add its receptionist.
