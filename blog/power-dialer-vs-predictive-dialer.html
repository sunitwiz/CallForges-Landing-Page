Power dialer vs predictive dialer: what's the difference?

A power dialer calls one lead at a time per rep, so the rep is on the line for every answer. A predictive dialer rings more leads than there are free agents, based on a guess of how many will answer. Predictive dialers suit large call centers, and power dialers suit most sales teams.

What is the difference between a power dialer and a predictive dialer?

Both are auto dialers. They work through a list for you, so nobody types numbers. The difference is how many calls they place at once and who decides that number.

A power dialer places one call per rep. When the call ends, it calls the next lead on the list. The rep sets the pace with a short pause between calls.

A predictive dialer places several calls per agent, and software decides how many. It watches how often calls are answered and how long agents spend on each call, then tries to time new calls so that someone answers just as an agent becomes free. When it guesses right, agents go from one conversation straight into the next. When it guesses wrong, a person answers and no agent is free to take the call.

How does a predictive dialer work?

A predictive dialer treats the call floor as a queue. It tracks numbers such as the share of calls that get answered, how long calls last and how many agents are online. From those it predicts when the next agent will be free and dials ahead of time, often several numbers per agent.

This only works with a crowd. With 50 agents, there is nearly always someone about to finish a call, so the predictions hold up. With two or three reps, the dialer has much less room to get it right, and a wrong guess means a lead picks up and hears nothing, or a recorded message.

Predictive dialers also hand over calls with very little warning. The agent hears a connection and starts talking, often with only a moment to see who is on the other end. That fits scripted, high-volume work such as collections or surveys. It fits less well for sales, where knowing who you are calling is half the job.

How does a power dialer work?

A power dialer keeps the rep in the loop. In CallForges, you click Start Campaign and the dialer calls the lead at the top of your list. Your own line places the call, so you hear it ring. When the lead answers, the strip shows Connected and their contact page opens.

After the call, the strip shows Wrapping up… with a countdown to the next call. Click the countdown to skip it, click +10s for more time, or click Log outcome to fill in the post-call form while the countdown waits. With Auto-advance on, the next lead rings when the countdown ends.

If a call reaches a voicemail box, pick one of your recorded messages and click Drop voicemail. The message plays into the box and the campaign moves on. You can read more in What is a power dialer?

The result is a steady rhythm. The rep is never surprised by a call, the lead never answers to silence, and nobody spends time typing numbers.

Which one is easier to keep compliant?

The power dialer, by a wide margin. The rules on abandoned calls exist mostly because of dialers that ring more people than there are agents to talk to.

In the US, the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule treats a call as abandoned when the person who answers is not connected to a sales representative within two seconds of their greeting. Its safe harbor allows no more than 3 percent of answered calls to be abandoned per campaign over each 30-day period, and it expects a recorded message with the seller's name and number when nobody is free. The FCC sets a similar limit for telemarketing calls in 47 CFR 64.1200. The FTC explains how this applies to predictive dialers in Complying with the Telemarketing Sales Rule.

In the UK, Ofcom's policy on persistent misuse covers silent and abandoned calls, expects an information message within two seconds when no agent is free, and treats silent calls as the more harmful kind.

A predictive dialer has to be tuned and measured to stay inside those limits. A power dialer places one call at a time from the rep's own line, so the rep is there when the lead answers. Consent, do-not-call registers and calling hours apply to both.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules depend on who you call, what you sell and where. Consent, calling hours and do-not-call rules are your responsibility under the CallForges Terms.

Which dialer should your team use?

For most sales teams, the power dialer. Pick it when:

  • Your team is small, from one founder up to a few dozen reps.
  • You call warm leads, trial users, booked callbacks or existing customers, who answer often.
  • You call consumers in the US or the UK.
  • Your reps need a few seconds to see who they are calling before the lead says hello.

A predictive dialer makes more sense for a large call center with many agents on the same script, calling big lists where answer rates are low.

There is also a middle option for small teams with big cold lists. A parallel dialer rings a few leads at once for a single rep and connects the first one who answers. You choose how many lines ring, so the rep stays in control. It fits business-to-business lists where most calls go unanswered. Our power dialer vs parallel dialer guide covers when to use each.

How do you set up a power dialer in CallForges?

CallForges has two dialer modes: the power dialer and the parallel dialer. You pick one per campaign.

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Power Dialer, or Parallel Dialer for a large cold B2B list.
  3. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see, and set the Cooldown duration between two calls.
  4. Under Audience, choose Select a list or Upload leads to import a CSV file.
  5. Turn on Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists and Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours, then click Create Campaign.
  6. Open the campaign, allow the browser to use your microphone, and click Start Campaign.

The dialer type can't be changed after the campaign is created. To move leads to the other dialer, tick them in the campaign, click Create campaign, and change the dialer type in the follow-up form. Create a campaign covers every option.
