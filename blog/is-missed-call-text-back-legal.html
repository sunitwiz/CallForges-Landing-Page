Is missed call text back legal? US, UK and EU rules

Missed call text back is generally allowed in the US, UK and EU when the text answers the person's call and sells nothing. In the US, check three things: the TCPA's consent rules, STOP replies and your number's carrier registration (A2P 10DLC); in the UK and EU, the strict consent rules are aimed at marketing. Say someone rings your HVAC company at 7pm, gets voicemail, and a text asks what they need. That's a reply to their call. Add "10% off your first service" and it's marketing, which is where most of these rules bite.

The US rules: what the TCPA says about texts

The Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) is the federal law, and the FCC's rules under it apply to any business calling or texting phones in the US. The FCC treats a text as a call for TCPA purposes (FCC 23-107, 2023). Under its rules, a call to a cell phone made with an autodialer or a prerecorded voice needs the person's prior express consent, and prior express written consent if it advertises or is telemarketing (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(1) and (a)(2), eCFR, 2026).

That rule covers your text back only if the system sending it counts as an autodialer. The FCC's rules define one as equipment that can store or produce numbers using a random or sequential number generator and dial them (64.1200(f)(2)), and the Supreme Court read the law the same way (Facebook v. Duguid, 2021). A text back goes to the number that just rang you. The rules don't say whether calling a business counts as consent to a reply, though, and we wouldn't build on either point.

A person can sue for $500 per violation, and a court can triple that when the violation was willful or knowing (47 U.S.C. 227(b)(3), 2023 edition). So we'd write the text as a reply about their call, with no offer in it, which keeps it away from the stricter rule for marketing.

Do Not Call and quiet hours

The National Do Not Call Registry and the 8am to 9pm calling window apply to telephone solicitations (64.1200(c)), and the FCC has confirmed the registry covers texts as well (FCC 23-107, 2023). A telephone solicitation is a call or message meant to encourage a purchase. The definition leaves out messages to someone who gave you permission, or to someone with an established business relationship, which includes asking about your products or services in the last three months (64.1200(f)(5) and (f)(15)). A reply about someone's call doesn't encourage a purchase.

State laws

States can add rules of their own. Florida's Telephone Solicitation Act covers sales calls, texts and voicemails to consumers in Florida, wherever the business sits. An unsolicited sales text sent with an automated system for selecting and dialing numbers needs prior express written consent, and a text sent at the person's express request, or to someone you already do business with, doesn't count as unsolicited. Before suing over sales texts, a person has to reply STOP, and the sender then has 15 days to stop (Fla. Stat. 501.059, 2026). Check the states where your callers live.

STOP replies: what the FCC requires

Once someone asks you to stop, you have at most 10 business days to honor it, for any text sent under the TCPA's consent rules (64.1200(a)(10) and (a)(11)). You can name a reply text as the way to opt out, as long as you accept any of seven words: "stop", "quit", "end", "revoke", "opt out", "cancel" and "unsubscribe". "Reply STOP to opt out" in each text then tells people how. If you don't name a way, any reasonable request counts.

You may send one text confirming the opt-out, with nothing to sell in it. Sent within five minutes, the FCC presumes it's covered by the person's earlier consent (64.1200(a)(12)). Then stop texting them.

The carriers' guidance asks for more. The CTIA Messaging Principles, the wireless industry's voluntary best practices, ask senders to say in the message how to opt out, to use STOP wording, and to act on plain requests such as "please opt me out" (CTIA, 2023). So have someone read the replies, and treat "don't text me again" as a STOP.

A2P 10DLC: the carriers' rule

US carriers block business texts from a local number that isn't registered with them. That's carrier policy, set by the mobile networks rather than by law, and it covers any business texting from an ordinary 10-digit US number through software. You register your business and a campaign: what you'll text, to whom, and how people agree to get it.

CTIA's guidance sorts texts by the consent each kind needs (CTIA, 2023):

Kind of textWhat it isConsent expected
ConversationalThe customer texts first and you replyNone beyond their text
InformationalThe customer gives you their number and asks to hear from youTheir permission, spoken or written
PromotionalA sales or marketing messageWritten permission

A missed call text back sits between the first two: the customer reached out first, by phone, and your text is the first message. Describe it in your registration as it is: people call your number, and you text them back about their call. Name your business in each sample message and end it with "Reply STOP to opt out". The UK and the EU

In the UK, the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) apply to anyone sending marketing texts to people there. The ICO, the UK regulator, says you must not text marketing to individuals without their specific consent, apart from a "soft opt-in" for your own previous customers, and every message needs a way to opt out. Sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals; companies don't (ICO, 2026). Service messages, sent for administrative or customer service reasons with nothing promotional, aren't direct marketing, but one with a marketing element counts as marketing (ICO, 2025). We'd keep a text back on the service side of that line.

In the EU, Article 13 of the ePrivacy Directive applies to anyone sending marketing texts to people there: direct marketing by electronic mail, a term that covers texts, needs the person's prior consent. A business may market its own similar products to customers whose details it got in a sale, if each message offers a free, easy way to object (Directive 2002/58/EC, 2002). Each EU country writes this into its own law, so check the country you're texting.

This is general information, not legal advice.

Missed call text back: legal questions

Can a missed call text include a coupon?

CTIA's guidance says adding a call to action, such as a coupon code, may move an informational text into the promotional category, which expects written permission (CTIA, 2023). Send the offer later, once the person has agreed to hear from you.

Can I send a missed call text at night?

The federal 8am to 9pm window is for telephone solicitations (64.1200(c)(1)), and a reply about someone's call isn't trying to sell them anything. State laws can add their own rules for sales texts, so a text with an offer in it is a different question.
