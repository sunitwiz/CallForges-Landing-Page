How to ask customers for Google reviews after every call

A pipe bursts under a kitchen sink on a Saturday morning. The plumber has it fixed by lunch, and the customer calls him a lifesaver. Three weeks later there's still no Google review, because nobody asked while the morning was fresh. The fix is one short text the same day with your direct Google review link, sent to every customer you helped, once.

CallForges team · 20 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • Ask the same day, in one short text with your direct review link, and don't chase it with reminders.
  • Ask everyone you served. Google bans asking only the happy customers, and it bans any reward for a review.
  • A US number needs carrier registration (A2P 10DLC) before it can text, so start that first.

Why happy customers don't leave reviews

They mean to, and then the day moves on. Once the pipe is fixed the problem is gone, and a review is a favor with no deadline. To post one, they also have to find you on Google and be signed in to a Google Account (Google Business Profile Help, 2026). We'd ask by text, with a link they can tap on the phone they called you from.

On the day, they remember the plumber's name and the water on the floor. Three weeks later it's "a plumber came out", if they write anything.

When to send it, and which calls to skip

Send it the same day, a few hours after the work is done. A support desk that fixes the problem on the call can text two hours later; a plumber whose call-outs take a morning might wait four.

Calls with no job behind them shouldn't get it: a wrong number, a supplier, someone booking a visit for next week. A call nobody answered needs a different text, one that asks what they need (missed call text back).

A complaint call shouldn't get "how did we do?" two hours later either. Skip a call because it wasn't a finished job, never a customer because they might be unhappy: they should have had the same text after their job as everyone else. Sending it only to people who sounded pleased is review gating, which Google bans.

Send one text, with no reminders. A customer who read it and didn't review has made a choice, and Google tells businesses not to pressure customers into reviews.

What to say when asking for a Google review

Keep it short and about their job:

  • Your business name and their first name up front, so it doesn't read like spam.
  • One line about the job, so they know which visit you mean.
  • The review link and, in the US, "Reply STOP to opt out".

Leave out stars, and don't ask them to name your plumber: Google tells businesses not to request specific content (Google Maps content policy, 2026). Drop "if you were happy with us" too, since it invites only the happy ones.

Aim for one 160-character text; the link takes room, so keep the words short. An emoji cuts that to 70, and each extra part is charged (SMS length and sending rules). Three examples, with your name and link in the brackets:

  • "Hi Sam, it's Dave at [Business]. Glad we got that pipe sorted today. A Google review would help us a lot: [link] Reply STOP to opt out"
  • "Hi Priya, thanks for calling [Business] today. We'd love to hear how we did on Google: [link] Reply STOP to opt out"
  • "Thanks for choosing [Business], Mark. A quick Google review helps other people find us: [link] Reply STOP to opt out"

How to get your direct Google review link

Google's steps (Google Business Profile Help, 2026):

  1. Go to your Business Profile.
  2. Select Read reviews, then Get more reviews.
  3. Select Copy to copy the review link.

Text it to yourself and tap it before customers get it.

Review gating, incentives and what the rules allow

Review gating means asking customers how they felt and sending only the happy ones to Google, often with a star-rating page that sends low scores to a private form. Google's Maps content policy, a platform rule, says merchants may not "discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers". It also bans offering "payment, discounts, free goods and/or services" for posting any review, and Google removes incentivized reviews (Google Maps content policy, 2026).

The law is narrower. The FTC's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465, has applied since October 21, 2024 (eCFR, 2026). For a business asking for reviews, it bans:

  • writing or buying fake reviews (§ 465.2)
  • rewards conditioned on a review being positive, or negative (§ 465.4)
  • groundless threats, intimidation or knowingly false accusations to stop or remove a review, and hiding bad reviews on your own site while implying you show all or most of them (§ 465.7)

The rule doesn't mention review gating, and a discount for any honest review falls outside § 465.4, though Google still bans it. The FTC's guide for marketers is advice, not law, and it says: "Don't ask for reviews only from customers you think will leave positive ones" (FTC, 2022).

You may ask everyone you served, the same way. To find out who's unhappy, use a survey and call them back, and send them the link like everyone else.

Do you need consent to text a review request?

The US federal law is the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), and the FCC's rules under it require prior express consent for autodialed or prerecorded calls to a cell phone, and prior express written consent when the call advertises or is telemarketing. The same rules cover texts, and people can withdraw consent by replying "stop" or similar words (47 CFR 64.1200(a), eCFR, 2026).

For a review text to a customer who rang you today, it depends on the situation: how your system sends it, whether it reads as marketing, and your state's laws. We'd keep the text to the review, with no offer, and stop when someone replies STOP.

Registration is carrier policy: US carriers block business texts from an unregistered local number. You register your business and an A2P 10DLC campaign, with your review text among the sample messages, and the carriers' review usually takes 1 to 3 weeks (Register for SMS in the US).

This is general information, not legal advice.

How to send a Google review text after calls in CallForges

In CallForges the review text is a step in a call workflow:

  1. In Workflows, open the workflow of the number your customers call. If the number still uses the Direct line, click Duplicate it to make changes.
  2. Click + on the Answered path of the Ring users or teams step, add a Schedule rule, and set Send after to, say, 2 Hours (up to 24 hours, or 30 Days).
  3. Add Send SMS after it and set Send SMS from to Incoming call number.
  4. Write the Message with your link, and use Insert field to add the caller's First name with a fallback such as "there".
  5. Click Publish, since a draft never sends. For a copy, click Numbers and add your number.

The text goes two hours after the call ends, even if the customer hangs up first. The delay ignores your opening hours, so a call that ends at 8pm texts at 10pm; for late calls, use the daytime path of a Time rule. Each answered call sends one text, so someone who rings three times about one job gets three, which suits businesses where one call means one job. If suppliers call a separate number, keep the text off its workflow.

To learn how calls went, put a Post-call survey before the text. Callers rate the call from 1 to 5 on their keypad, and all who reach the Send SMS get the link, whatever they pressed. A customer who replies STOP isn't texted again from any of your numbers until they reply START (SMS opt-outs).
