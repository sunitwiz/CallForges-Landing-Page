How to set up a parallel dialer: lines, lists and pausing

It's 2pm. You've got a two-hour call block and 300 old leads. On a normal dialer you dial, sit through six rings, hit voicemail and dial again, and most of the block goes to waiting. A parallel dialer rings several leads at once and puts you through to whoever picks up first. In CallForges it's one campaign form. Ring two or three lines on your biggest cold list, and let the round finish before you take a break.

CallForges team · 18 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • Start with two or three lines. Once someone answers, the dialer hangs up on everyone still ringing, so each extra line can burn one more lead per conversation.
  • Save it for big cold lists where most calls go unanswered. Warm leads and consumer lists do better on a power dialer.
  • For a break, let the ringing phones finish if you can take one more call. Pausing never cuts a call you're on.

How many lines should you ring at once?

Start with two. Go to three after a session or two, once you've checked how many leads got cut. Past three, we'd want a list full of voicemail and dead numbers.

You can talk to one person at a time. Once someone answers, the dialer hangs up every phone still ringing. Those leads hear a ring or two and then nothing, and CallForges marks them cancelled.

As an example, say one in ten of your 300 leads picks up, so you get about 30 conversations. Ring three lines and each of those cuts up to two other leads: up to 60 people who got a ring and a hang-up. At five lines that's up to 120, two in five of your list. With re-dial off, the default, they're out for the rest of this campaign.

Sometimes two leads answer at the same moment. You get the first, and the second picks up to a call that ends on them. Abandoned-call rules target that second lead, and the higher your connect rate, the more of those calls you make. For cold business-to-business data, where most calls go unanswered, parallel dialing wins. Warm leads belong on the power dialer; Power dialer vs parallel dialer compares the two.

You can set re-dial so cut leads go back to the bottom of the list. We'd leave it off at two lines and call them later from a follow-up campaign. With three or more lines and a short list, let them come back once. The cost is that someone who picked up as the line dropped may hear from you again soon. If you finish a session with a pile of cancelled leads, ring one line fewer on your next campaign.

Load your biggest, coldest list

Most of your afternoon on a cold business-to-business list is idle time, rings nobody answers. That's the list to load: with three phones ringing together, more of the block is talk time.

The dialer works top to bottom, so put the leads you want most at the top. A CSV straight from Excel is fine, as long as it has a phone number column.

Set calling hours in your leads' own time; a US number uses its area code's time zone. If a lead's turn comes outside those hours, the dialer skips them for the rest of the campaign. Start your block while your leads are at their desks, or a chunk of the list drops out before you've said a word.

The rules on hung-up calls

These are laws. Both US rules are about sales calls, and both count a call as abandoned when nobody comes on the line within two seconds of the person's hello. The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule limits abandoned telemarketing calls, but most business-to-business calls are exempt. The FCC's rule, 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(7), caps them at 3% of answered telemarketing calls per campaign over 30 days, and its text has no business-to-business exemption, so a cold B2B list still counts. In the UK, Ofcom, the telecoms regulator, has legal powers against persistent misuse of a phone line, and its policy statement on using them counts a call hung up as the person picks up as a silent call. Ofcom wrote it mainly about calls to consumers.

So the risk varies with who you call, and consumer lists carry the most. We'd keep them on the power dialer for that reason: it rings one lead at a time, so two people can't answer at once. It's our advice; no rule requires it. Neither dialer makes a list compliant by itself, so check your lists with counsel. Is parallel dialing legal? covers each rule. Set it up in CallForges

You'll need a paid plan: on the free trial, the Campaigns page reads Campaigns start with your plan. Your role needs Create and edit campaigns to set it up and Run campaigns to start it; owners, admins and members have both. If you have no active number, the form shows Get a number. Allow your microphone, and keep the CallForges tab open while you dial.

  1. Open Campaigns, click Create campaign, and click Untitled Campaign to rename it.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Parallel Dialer. You can't change it later, so decide per list.
  3. Set Number of parallel calls. We'd start at two.
  4. Set Re-dial cancelled leads. Never is the default. A number sends a cut lead back to the bottom of the list up to that many times.
  5. Pick a Voicemail drop if you've recorded one.
  6. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see, and set Cooldown duration between two calls in seconds.
  7. Under Audience, choose Select a list, or Upload leads for a CSV.
  8. At the bottom, turn on Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists and Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours.
  9. Set the Active Days, Start Time and End Time, then click Create Campaign.

The campaign opens as a Draft, and nothing dials until you click Start Campaign. To change the dial order later, click Edit and drag rows.

Then turn on Auto machine detection in Settings → Dialer settings. The voicemail drop plays only with it on. The machine hears your message before the dialer moves on, and the lead shows Voicemail dropped. With it off, a machine counts as an answer. The dialer puts you through to a greeting and hangs up on the leads still ringing, one of whom might have been a person.

Each field is in How the parallel dialer works and Create a campaign, with screenshots.

Running your first session

Your own line connects first, so the dialer rings leads only while you're ready to talk. The session bar shows how many lines are live. If one rings out, the dialer calls the next lead in its place and the batch stays full.

Someone picks up and you're connected, with their contact page open, so you know who you're talking to before you've finished saying hello. Hang up and the post-call form opens for the outcome and your notes. A countdown runs to the next round. Skip it when you're ready, or add time when the notes run long.

Pause now, or after this round?

Let the round finish if you can take one more conversation. Stop now if you can't, say your manager's at your desk. Either way, pausing never cuts a call you're on: hit pause mid-conversation and the campaign pauses without asking.

Hit pause while lines are ringing and you pick how to stop:

Your choiceThe phones still ringingPick it if
Pause nowThe dialer hangs up on them and marks those leads cancelled.You have to go.
Pause after this roundThey ring out. If someone answers, you're connected. Nothing new dials.You can take one more call.
End the campaignThe dialer hangs up every line, your call included, and marks the leads nobody called as skipped. You can't start it again.You're done with the list.

Our default is after this round, because stopping now burns the leads already ringing. To carry on, resume the campaign and dialing picks up at the next lead nobody has called.
