10DLC registration fees and how long approval takes

You want appointment reminders going out from your US business number by the first of next month, and someone just told you texting needs "10DLC". Here's the short version. For a typical small business, 10DLC registration costs about $20 to submit plus $10 a month, and the carriers' review usually takes one to three weeks. A rejected campaign costs the review fee again and restarts the wait, so the first submission is the one to get right.

CallForges team · 27 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • The fees are the carriers' own: a one-time business fee, a review fee per campaign and a monthly fee per campaign.
  • Approval usually takes one to three weeks, and you can't change a campaign while it's in review.
  • Rejections are where the money and time go. Clear samples and a clear consent description avoid most of them.

What 10DLC registration costs

The US carriers set the 10DLC fees, and texting platforms pass them on. Some add their own charges on top, so ask what a platform adds. In CallForges, the carriers' fees are paid on your card when you submit:

FeeWhen you pay itPrice
Business registrationOnce per workspace, with your first campaign$4.50
Campaign reviewEach new campaign, and each resubmission of a rejected one$15
Monthly campaign feeEvery month for each approved campaign, until you delete it$10 a month ($2.40 for a low-volume mixed campaign)
Business vettingOnce, only if you'll send more than 2,000 texts a day$41.50

So a first campaign costs $19.50 to submit, or $61 with business vetting. Each later campaign or resubmission costs $15. The texts themselves are charged separately, per message, at the rate for the country they reach.

A worked example: a plumbing company with one customer-care campaign pays $19.50 in month one, then $10 a month. Over a year that's $19.50 plus twelve monthly fees, so $139.50 if the campaign is approved the first time and stays approved all year.

How long does 10DLC approval take?

The carriers' review usually takes one to three weeks. You can't speed it up, and you can't edit a campaign while it's in review, so the clock is the thing to plan around.

The wait has two parts. Your business has to be registered first, and in CallForges that starts with an approved Business profile, which usually clears in 24 to 48 hours. Then the campaign goes to the carriers. If they reject it, you fix it and the review starts again from the beginning.

Our advice: register the week you get the number, even if you won't text for a month. A campaign that sits approved for two weeks costs you nothing extra, and a campaign you submit the day before a launch can miss it by three weeks.

Do you need business vetting?

Most small businesses don't. Without vetting, the carriers let a business send up to about 2,000 texts a day. Vetting is an independent check of your business that gives it a trust score, and the score sets how many texts you can send a day above that.

Pick vetting only if you really will send more than 2,000 texts a day, such as a large reminder list or a busy multi-location business. It's asked once, before your business is registered, so think about next year's volume too.

How to avoid paying the review fee twice

Every rejection costs another $15 and another wait. The carriers say why they rejected a campaign, and most fixes come down to being specific:

  • Write sample messages exactly as customers will get them, with your business name and "Reply STOP to opt out" in each.
  • Describe consent concretely: which form, at which web address, and the wording people agree to. "Customers opt in" is too vague.
  • Make the description match the samples. If the samples are appointment reminders, don't describe a marketing program.
  • Check the content isn't on the carriers' banned list, such as cannabis, high-risk loans or gambling.

If your sign-up form doesn't mention texts at all, fix the form first. The CTIA Messaging Principles, the wireless industry's own voluntary guidance, expect consent before texting and written agreement for promotional texts (CTIA, 2023). That's carrier policy and best practice, not a statute, but it's what reviewers look for.

This is general information, not legal advice.

When you get money back

Only a fee the carriers never take is refunded. In CallForges:

  • If the carriers reject your business registration, so it's never created, that fee and your campaigns' review fees go back to your card.
  • If you delete a campaign before the carriers take it for review, its review fee is refunded.
  • A campaign the carriers reject after taking it for review keeps its review fee.

Deleting an approved campaign stops its monthly fee, and its numbers stop texting. If your plan ends, your numbers are released and every campaign is deleted, and registering again later means a new review fee and another one to three weeks.
