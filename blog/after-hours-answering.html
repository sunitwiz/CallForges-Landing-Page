After-hours answering for small businesses

It's 11pm, and a tenant is calling the property manager's office line because water is coming through her kitchen ceiling. Earlier that evening the same line got a parking question and a hang-up at the beep. After-hours answering decides where those calls go while you're closed: voicemail, the person on call, an answering service or an AI agent. For most small businesses we'd mix them, so the leak reaches a person and the parking question waits for morning.

How after-hours answering works

Your phone system needs two answers from you: when you're open, and where a call goes when you're not. Outside open hours, every call takes a second path you set up once, whether it arrives at 3am on a Sunday or on Thanksgiving morning.

That second path can split. A caller with an emergency presses a key, or dials a separate number you gave them, and reaches the person on call. Everyone else gets voicemail or an AI agent. Your phone system can't tell a burst pipe from a parking question until the caller tells it, so ask early and in plain words.

Emergencies vs routine calls: who should answer at night?

We'd send emergencies to a person and let an AI agent or voicemail take everything else. A tenant with water through the ceiling needs someone who can shut a valve or send a plumber. An AI agent can answer her questions and book a callback, but it can't stop the leak. A question about your opening hours, or "can you come next Tuesday?", can wait for morning or get its answer from your documents.

Before you choose any of it, write down what counts as an emergency. One Hacker News commenter described how his wife's medical office sorted after-hours calls into three groups: emergencies were told to call an ambulance, urgent ones got a prescription or directions to an after-hours clinic, and the rest waited for the office to open (Hacker News, 2019). A plumber's list might be a leak you can't stop or a sewer backup. Read the list in your after-hours greeting, and callers sort themselves before anyone's phone rings.

The cost of this setup is sleep. The person holding the on-call phone gets woken up, sometimes for a dripping tap a caller swore was urgent, and in a small business that person is often the owner. A man who ran an internet provider in the 1990s wrote on Hacker News that he paid an answering service to take calls after hours and still carried "the dreaded pager". He'd "take the 2AM calls usually", being the owner (Hacker News, 2014). Rotate the phone among people who can fix the problem, and pay them for the weeks they carry it. If you charge an after-hours call-out fee, say so in the greeting: a caller with a dripping tap hears the price and can choose to wait.

Callers who won't leave a voicemail

Voicemail costs nothing, but some callers won't use it. Under a post about an AI receptionist for a mechanic shop, one commenter wrote that "some people will hang up once the mailbox message starts", and added that some hang up on an AI chatbot too (Hacker News, 2026).

Unless they hide their number, a caller who hangs up at the beep still leaves it in your call history. Call those numbers back first thing, before they phone the next business on the list. Keep the greeting short and give the two facts a caller needs: when you reopen, and how to reach someone for an emergency.

Holidays and special dates

A plan built around weekday hours treats a holiday as a normal day and rings an empty office. This year Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. Christmas Day falls on Friday, December 25, and New Year's Day on Friday, January 1, 2027, so each gives a three-day weekend (OPM federal holidays, 2026).

  • Treat a holiday like a night, with the same paths for emergencies and routine calls.
  • Change the greeting to say when you reopen: "We're closed for Thanksgiving and back Friday at 8am."
  • Name the person on call for a long weekend before the week starts.

Snow days and sick days need the same plan with less warning, so decide now how you'll close the phones at short notice.

How to set up after-hours answering in CallForges

In CallForges a call workflow decides what happens on each of your numbers, and business hours and voicemail come with every plan. Build a call workflow has the full guide.

  1. Open Workflows, click New workflow, pick Business hours + voicemail and click Use template. It rings the number's owner Monday to Friday, 9 to 5, and takes a voicemail At any other time. Set the Time rule to your own hours and time zone.
  2. Add a Date rule for each holiday. It sends a holiday or other special date down a different path from Business days, so Thanksgiving goes where your nights go.
  3. Choose what happens At any other time: Voicemail with the greeting and mailbox you pick, Forward call to the on-call mobile (with its country code), or a hand-off to the AI agent.
  4. Give emergencies their own way to a person. An IVR menu can play "press 1 for an emergency" and send that key to a Forward call. Or give a second number its own workflow that forwards to whoever's on call.
  5. Click Save draft, then Publish. Click Numbers, pick your numbers and click Save numbers.

A Ring users or teams step skips anyone outside their working hours, so a night call that only rings your team goes straight to its Unanswered path. Put a voicemail or a forward on that path.
