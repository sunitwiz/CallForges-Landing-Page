How much does an AI receptionist cost?

You've decided you're done missing calls after 5pm, and now you're staring at three pricing pages that each count differently. What an AI receptionist costs depends on how it's priced, per minute, per call or as a monthly bundle, and on how many minutes your callers actually talk. Work out your monthly minutes first, then compare. As an example, 40 after-hours calls a month comes to under $15 in agent minutes on CallForges.

CallForges team · 26 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • Most of the cost is the minutes the AI spends talking, so start by counting your calls and their length.
  • Watch the extras: phone numbers, transfers, setup fees and what happens when you go over a bundle.
  • A part-time person costs more per hour but handles judgment calls. Many teams use both.

How AI receptionists are priced

There are three common ways vendors charge, and the same month of calls can cost very different amounts under each.

Pricing modelHow it worksGood forWatch out for
Per minuteYou pay for the minutes the AI talksShort calls, uneven monthsLong calls add up
Per callA flat price for each call answeredLong callsPaying full price for a 20-second hang-up
Monthly bundleA set fee covers a number of minutes or callsSteady volumeOverage prices, and unused minutes that expire

Ask every vendor the same questions: what counts as a minute or a call, what you pay after a transfer to a person, and whether a phone number is included. Those answers matter more than the headline price.

Work out your own cost

Pull last month's call history and count the calls nobody answered, and when they came in. Those are the calls an AI receptionist would take. Then multiply by an average length; three minutes is a fair guess for a question or a message.

Costs people forget

  • Phone numbers. Some services charge for the number the AI answers on. On CallForges every user gets one free local number, and extra numbers start at $5 a month.
  • Transfers. When the AI hands a caller to your team, some services keep billing AI minutes. On CallForges, the rest of the call uses normal calling minutes.
  • Setup and training. Ask whether someone charges to load your FAQs, and who updates them when your prices change.
  • Your own time. The AI knows only what you give it, so budget an hour to write and test its answers.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Do I pay for calls that hang up in the first few seconds?
  2. Is there a minimum monthly spend or a contract length?
  3. What happens when I go over my bundle: a higher rate, or does it stop answering?
  4. Can callers reach a person, and does that cost extra?
  5. Can I try it on my own number before I pay?

A vendor that answers all five plainly on its pricing page is easier to budget for than one that needs a sales call to tell you.

AI receptionist vs a human: the cost comparison

The US median pay for receptionists was $18.27 an hour in May 2025 (BLS, 2025). At 40 hours a week, that's about $3,170 a month in wages, before taxes and benefits, and they still go home at five.

An AI receptionist is far cheaper per hour and works nights and weekends. A person is better at quotes, upset callers and anything you haven't written down. We'd keep people on the daytime calls and let the AI take the hours nobody's in, which is where the cost difference is biggest. Is it worth it?

It's worth it when the calls it catches would otherwise go to voicemail or a competitor. Put a value on one new customer and compare. If one job from 40 missed calls is worth $200, a month of $14.40 in agent minutes pays for itself many times over. If your missed calls are mostly sales pitches, it isn't worth much.
