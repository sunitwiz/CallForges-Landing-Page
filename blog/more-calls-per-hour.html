How to make more calls per hour

It's 3pm, and a rep has blocked an hour for calls. She finds the next row, types the number and sits through five rings. Then she reads the same voicemail script for the twentieth time and types up the call. By 4pm she's had three conversations. You make more calls per hour by cutting the time between conversations. Let a power dialer do the dialing, drop voicemails in one click, and keep your notes to an outcome and a line.

CallForges team · 19 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • Most of a calling hour goes to the gaps between conversations. Shrink the gaps and leave the talking alone.
  • On warm leads, a power dialer with a short pause beats a parallel dialer, because everyone who answers reaches you.
  • Count conversations per hour as well as dials. A pace that leaves no time for notes costs you the follow-up.

How many calls per hour can a rep make?

It depends on your list more than on your rep. A list where one person in three picks up means more talk time and fewer dials than a list where most phones ring out. So measure your own: calls per hour is dials divided by the hours you spent dialing. Count only the hours in your call block, which is what people mean by calls per productive hour.

Take one example hour, by hand and on a power dialer. Say finding the lead and typing the number takes 20 seconds, phones ring for 25 seconds, one dial in three reaches a voicemail greeting, and one in ten reaches a person for four minutes.

The talk time stayed the same. The dialer took out the typing and most of the note-taking, which left ringing as the biggest cost: about 23 minutes of the hour. Your own guesses will move the totals, so use the maths to find your biggest gap.

What to cut first

We'd go in this order.

  1. Stop dialing by hand. It's the easiest win, and nobody misses it.
  2. Shorten the wrap-up. Type notes while you talk, then pick an outcome and add a line. Typing the whole note after you hang up is where the minutes go.
  3. Stop reading voicemails out. Record the message once and drop it, so a greeting costs you a click.
  4. Call when people answer. On a business list, a dial at 7pm rings as long as one at 10am, and fewer people are at their desks to pick up. Calling at better hours adds conversations more than dials.
  5. Clean the list. A dead or wrong number costs a full ring or a failed call, and you learn nothing from it.

The trade-off sits in step two. Every second you take out of the wrap-up comes out of the notes, and you build next week's follow-up list from those notes. We'd rather a rep make 50 dials with an outcome on each call than 60 with gaps. If reps start skipping outcomes to hit a dials target, lower the target.

Power or parallel: which makes more calls per hour?

A parallel dialer makes more dials per hour, because it rings several leads at once and goes after those 23 minutes of ringing. On a warm list we'd still pick the power dialer.

Once someone answers on a parallel dialer, it hangs up on the leads still ringing. On a cold list where nine in ten calls ring out, you lose little. On a list of demo no-shows or renewals, those cut leads are people who would have picked up. Sometimes two answer at once, and the second hears a hang-up. A power dialer rings one lead at a time, so everyone who answers reaches you.

Keep the power dialer for lists where people answer, and put the big cold lists on a parallel dialer. Power dialer vs parallel dialer compares the two, and How to set up a parallel dialer covers its settings.

Set it up and measure it in CallForges

Campaigns start with a paid plan; on the free trial the Campaigns page reads Campaigns start with your plan. Your role needs Create and edit campaigns to set one up and Run campaigns to start it. Owners, admins and members have both.

  1. Open Campaigns, click Create campaign, and under Dialer Type choose Power Dialer.
  2. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see, and set Cooldown duration between two calls in seconds. We'd start short and let reps add time when they need it.
  3. Under Audience, click Select a list, or Upload leads for a CSV. The dialer works from the top down, so put the warmest leads first.
  4. Turn on Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours and set the Active Days, Start Time and End Time. These are the lead's own hours.
  5. Click Create Campaign, allow your microphone, and click Start Campaign.

Start inside your leads' hours. A lead whose turn comes outside them shows Out of timezone, and the dialer skips them for the rest of that campaign. Calling hours are also a legal limit. In the US, the FCC's rule bars sales calls to consumers before 8am or after 9pm, in the called person's local time (47 CFR 64.1200(c)(1), eCFR, 2026). The pace settings sit in the campaign's Settings, under Dialing behavior. Keep Auto-advance on so the next lead rings by itself. Wrap-up time and Cooldown between calls set the countdown after each call. A shorter Ring timeout gives up sooner on calls nobody answers, but cut it too far and you'll hang up on people reaching for the phone.

On the call, use Note to type as you talk. After you hang up, the strip shows Wrapping up… and the countdown, Next call in … secs. If your workspace has the post-call form turned on, pick a Disposition and finish the note there. Log outcome opens the form and holds the next call until you close it. Click the countdown (Skip Cooldown) to dial now, or +10s when a note runs long.

At a voicemail greeting, drop your recording in one click and move on. On a parallel campaign the drop plays by itself, as long as Auto machine detection is on in Settings → Dialer settings. The full steps are in Create a campaign and Start, pause, resume or stop a campaign.

Read the numbers

Watch dials per hour for the pace and conversations per hour for the result. Owners and admins find both on the Dashboard.

  • On the Team tab, the leaderboard shows each user's Dials, Conversations and Dialing time, which runs from the start to the end of their campaign sessions. Divide dials by dialing time in hours to get calls per productive hour. Dials counts manual calls too, so it reads high for a rep who also dials by hand.
  • On the Outbound tab, Conversations are answered calls with over 60 seconds of talk. Call handling shows Avg wrap-up time and Voicemails left, and From dial to outcome can show Power calls alone.
  • Also on Outbound, Best time to call shows the answer rate by weekday and hour in the lead's local time. Cells with under 10 dials stay grey.

Read them in pairs. If Avg wrap-up time falls and Positive outcomes fall with it, the notes got too thin. If dials per hour climb and Conversations don't, look at the list or the hour, and move your call block to the hours with the best answer rate in Best time to call. Reps see their own Dials and Conversations against today's goal in My day, on Overview.
