Why firms look for a Smith.ai alternative

Smith.ai charges per call whether its AI answers (Smith.ai AI pricing, checked November 2026), its live receptionists do (Smith.ai receptionist pricing) or a mix of both (Smith.ai Hybrid pricing), so a 40-second question about your hours costs the same as a 9-minute intake.

Low-star reviewers on Trustpilot write most about leaving: notice periods, unrefunded prepaid months, charges after cancelling (Trustpilot, November 2026). Others say the AI sounded stiff, the per-call price added up, or spam used up the month. Smith.ai's AI plans do require 30 days' notice to cancel; they also filter known spam numbers and let you strike up to 10% of a cycle's calls as spam (Smith.ai AI pricing).

AI receptionist vs answering service: where your team comes in

Smith.ai and Ruby sell human receptionists; the other five here are AI only. Smith.ai, which sells both, says about 25% of AI-led calls usually need a live agent (Smith.ai Hybrid pricing).

With CallForges, that live agent is your own team. The AI voice agent hands any caller who asks to your team, and books a callback when your sources don't cover the question, so a person hears the details first. You pay $0.12 a minute for the agent's part of the call, and the rest uses normal calling minutes, not a receptionist's rate.

An AI agent earns its keep when most calls have a written answer and the lost ones come after 6pm (What is an AI receptionist?). An HVAC company can let it answer "do you cover my area?" at night and send a no-heat emergency to whoever's on call. Before you sign, check what gets billed (spam included), how you leave and whether you keep your number.

Smith.ai alternatives, AI and human

CallForges: our pick for small firms

CallForges is a calling platform with the AI voice agent built in. The agent answers 24/7 in 30+ languages, only from the sources you give it: help articles, web pages, files or text. It always says it's an automated assistant, hands the caller to your team when they ask, and books a callback when it can't answer instead of guessing. Your call routing decides when it picks up, so your team keeps the day and the agent takes nights and weekends. Plans start at $15 per user a month billed annually, and the agent comes with every paid plan at $0.12 a minute; Business and Scale include agent minutes, pooled, with 100 per user a month on Business (CallForges pricing). Every plan also has the power and parallel dialers, so your phones, your after-hours answering and your outbound calls sit on one bill.

Ruby: people only, from $250 for 50 minutes

Ruby puts live receptionists on your calls, billed by the minute. Its AI helps the receptionists and doesn't answer calls (Ruby AI). Plans start at $250 a month for 50 minutes (Ruby pricing, checked November 2026), extra minutes cost $3.30 to $5.40, and you can downgrade only after three billing periods on one plan (Ruby help center).

Goodcall: billed per caller, no free trial

Goodcall charges per unique caller a month, not counting spam or wrong numbers: $79 for 100 callers, $129 for 500, $299 for 2,000 (Goodcall pricing, checked November 2026). Our example costs $79 to $129, by the number of different callers. You get a demo agent instead of a free trial, and its FAQ lists no languages.

My AI Front Desk: $0.25 a minute past the plan

The main plan, $99 a month or $79 billed annually, covers 200 voice minutes plus chat, texts and a CRM (My AI Front Desk pricing, checked November 2026). Monthly credits add about 40 minutes, then each minute costs $0.25, so our example comes to about $270.

Dialzara: transfers and after-hours rules from $99

Dialzara starts at $29 a month for 60 minutes and runs to $349 for 1,000, with extra minutes at $0.35 to $0.48 (Dialzara pricing, checked November 2026). Our example fits the $349 plan. At $29, warm transfers, after-hours rules and calendar sync aren't included; they start on the $99 plan.

Rosie: English and Spanish, and going over moves you up a plan

Rosie answers every call in English and Spanish, at $49 for 250 minutes, $149 for 1,000 and $299 for 2,000 (Rosie pricing, checked November 2026). Our example fits $149, where calendar booking and warm transfers start. Going over puts you on the next plan up for at least two more billing periods (Rosie terms).

Prices are from each company's own pricing page, checked 25 November 2026. They change often.

Switching to CallForges

The free trial runs 14 days with no card, and includes a local US, UK, Canada or Australia number and 50 minutes each way (calls stop at 5 minutes until you add a card). The trial is for the phone system: set up your numbers and your workflow, and the AI voice agent starts answering once you choose a plan.

  1. Write down what callers ask, like hours and fees, and gather the help articles, web pages, files or text that answer them. The agent answers only from those.
  2. Open Workflows, click New workflow, pick Business hours + voicemail and click Use template. Set the Time rule to your hours (Build a call workflow).
  3. On the At any other time path, add the AI voice agent step from the CallForges AI section of the step picker. If you want an emergency line too, an IVR menu with an emergency key that forwards to the on-call phone can sit ahead of it.
  4. Click Publish, then Numbers, pick your number and click Save numbers.
  5. Open Phone Numbers and click Port number. A US number costs $10, charged once its port date is confirmed, and you can start during the trial. Your old service keeps working until the move completes (Port your number).
