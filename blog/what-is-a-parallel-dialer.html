What is a parallel dialer?

An SDR has 400 company main lines from an old trade-show list. On a one-line dialer, most of the hour goes to six rings and a voicemail greeting, over and over. A parallel dialer is an auto dialer that rings several of those leads at once and connects you to the first who picks up. It hangs up the other lines, so on a list where most calls go unanswered, more of your hour goes to talking.

CallForges team · 17 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • A parallel dialer rings a few leads at once for one rep, puts the first answer through and hangs up the rest.
  • It pays off on big cold business lists where most calls ring out. Warm leads and consumer lists belong on a power dialer.
  • When two people answer together, the second gets hung up on. US and UK rules target that call, so ring few lines.

How does a parallel dialer work?

Your own line connects first, so leads ring only while you're ready to talk. Then the dialer works the list in batches:

  1. It rings the next few leads together. You pick how many lines.
  2. When a line ends without an answer, it rings the next lead in its place, so the batch stays full.
  3. The first person who answers is put through to you, with their contact page open, and the dialer hangs up the other lines at once.
  4. After the call you log the outcome, a short countdown runs, and the next round starts.

On a one-line dialer you wait through each miss in turn; here the misses ring side by side. As an example, say one lead in ten picks up. One line at a time, you sit through about ten calls for each conversation. With three lines ringing together, you're talking after about four rounds.

The trade is preparation: you can't read up on a lead before you know who'll answer.

What happens when two leads answer at once?

You get the first, and the dialer hangs up on the second. From their side, they picked up and the line went dead.

CallForges marks that lead cancelled. It does the same to a lead still ringing when another answers first, and to the lines ringing when you pause right away.

By default, a cancelled lead isn't called again in that campaign, or you can send it back to the bottom of the list a set number of times. More lines and a list that answers well both mean more cuts, so we'd start at two lines, with re-dial off unless the list is short. If a session leaves a pile of cancelled leads on the campaign's list, ring one line fewer next time; How many lines should you ring at once? works through the maths.

Is parallel dialing legal?

It can be, and who you call decides most of it. These rules protect the person who answers and then gets hung up on.

United States: two federal rules

Both are law. Under the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule (16 CFR 310.4, as amended in 2024), a call is abandoned when a person answers and isn't connected to a sales representative within two seconds of finishing their greeting. Abandoning calls is prohibited, and the rule's safe harbor protects a seller only if it meets every part of it:

  • no more than 3 percent of calls answered by a person are abandoned, per campaign, over each 30-day period;
  • an unanswered call rings for at least 15 seconds or four rings;
  • when no rep is free within two seconds, a recorded message gives the seller's name and phone number;
  • records show all of this.

Most calls between a telemarketer and a business are exempt, but calls selling nondurable office or cleaning supplies are covered. The FTC's guide, Complying with the Telemarketing Sales Rule, also treats a call to a business line that pitches an employee something for personal use as covered.

The FCC's rule under the TCPA, 47 CFR 64.1200 (as amended in 2025), sets a similar 3 percent limit and two-second test for telemarketing calls, requires an identification and opt-out message, and bars hanging up an unanswered call before 15 seconds or four rings. Its text has no business-to-business exemption, so check it with counsel even for B2B lists.

United Kingdom: Ofcom's policy on silent and abandoned calls

This one is a regulator's enforcement policy, made under the Communications Act 2003. Ofcom's statement of policy on persistent misuse, which applies from 1 March 2017, treats silent and abandoned calls as misuse and says callers should make neither. A silent call includes one where the caller hangs up as soon as, or shortly after, the person picks up, with no message. An abandoned call is one where a recorded message plays because no agent is free. Ofcom sees silent calls as the more harmful kind.

If agents run short, Ofcom expects a message within two seconds that says who called and gives a number to ring to decline further calls. In picking cases, it may weigh:

  • the abandoned call rate;
  • calls cut off before they have rung for 15 seconds;
  • repeat calls to the same number within 72 hours, without a live agent guaranteed;
  • answering machine detection that mistakes a person for a machine.

The statement is written mainly about consumers, but Ofcom's powers cover harm to "someone else, especially consumers", and it sets out no exemption for business numbers.

What this means for a CallForges parallel campaign

Under those definitions, a second answer that the dialer hangs up is an abandoned call for the TSR and a silent call for Ofcom. A line still ringing when another lead answers is hung up at once, which can be before 15 seconds. We point the parallel dialer at business-to-business calling, where the TSR mostly does not apply, and we'd put consumer lists in the US or the UK on the power dialer, which keeps you on the line for every call. Whoever you call, set do-not-call and calling hours on the campaign. Consent, calling hours and do-not-call rules are your responsibility under the CallForges Terms.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules depend on who you call, what you sell and where. Check with a lawyer who knows telemarketing law before you run any auto dialer on consumer lists.

Who should use a parallel dialer?

Teams with a lot of numbers and a low chance that any one answers: SDRs on big business-to-business lists, agencies running outbound for clients, and founders who block an hour for prospecting.

Keep consumer lists in the US or the UK off it, for the reasons above, and warm leads too. Inbound leads and booked callbacks answer more, so they collide more, and a warm lead you hang up on is an expensive mistake. We decide per list: if most calls ring out and losing the odd lead to a hang-up is fine, parallel; if every lead matters, power.

Set up a parallel dialer in CallForges

Campaigns start with a paid plan. One user runs a parallel campaign at a time, with the CallForges tab open.

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Parallel Dialer. You can't change it later, so pick it per list.
  3. Set Number of parallel calls and Re-dial cancelled leads (Never is the default), and pick a Voicemail drop if you've recorded one.
  4. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see and set the Cooldown duration between two calls. Under Audience, pick a contact list or upload a CSV with a phone number column.
  5. Turn on Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists and Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours, then click Create Campaign.
  6. Open the campaign, allow your microphone, and click Start Campaign.

The session bar shows the live lines (3/3 lines), and cut leads show Cancelled. For a break, Pause now hangs up the ringing lines and Pause after this round lets them finish. The voicemail drop plays only with Auto machine detection on in Settings → Dialer settings, and the dialer moves on without you. With it off, a machine counts as an answer and is put through to you, and the dialer hangs up the leads still ringing.
