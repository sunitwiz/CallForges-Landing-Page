Christmas voicemail greeting examples for business

It's 3pm on Christmas Eve, the office is emptying, and nobody remembers who changed the voicemail last year. A Christmas voicemail greeting for business tells callers which days you're closed, the day and date you're back, and who to call if it can't wait. In 2026, Christmas Day and New Year's Day both fall on a Friday, so last year's recording names the wrong days. Record a short script per stretch of the break and let your phone system play each on its dates.

CallForges team · 27 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • If you're open some days of the break and shut on others, give each stretch its own short greeting.
  • In 2026, Christmas Day and New Year's Day are Fridays. UK offices also close Monday 28 December, Scottish ones Monday 4 January.
  • Switch the greetings on and off by date, so the Christmas message isn't playing on 12 January.

Which Christmas greeting plays on which day

If you shut early on Christmas Eve, close for the holidays themselves and run short hours in between, you need a greeting for each stretch. If you close straight through, one greeting does it, and our holiday voicemail greetings has it. The 2026 dates are from the US OPM federal holidays list and the UK's bank holidays page:

We'd rather record a short greeting per stretch than one long one that lists every date: a caller on the 29th only needs the 29th. The cost is ten minutes of recording and a second rule.

Christmas voicemail greeting examples, Christmas Eve to New Year's Day

Swap in your own name, hours and dates. Each reads aloud in under 30 seconds.

Christmas Eve: closing early

"Hi, you've reached [Business]. We closed at noon today for Christmas Eve and we're closed tomorrow. We open again on Monday, December 28, at 9am. Leave your name, number and what you need, and we'll return messages that morning in the order they came in. Merry Christmas from all of us."

Christmas Day voicemail greeting

"Merry Christmas, and thanks for calling [Business]. We're closed today, Friday, December 25, and back on Monday, December 28, from 9am. To check on an order, go to [website] any time. For anything else, leave a message and we'll call you back on Monday."

A UK office says Tuesday 29 December, as Monday 28 is a bank holiday.

Boxing Day and the bank holiday (UK)

"Thank you for calling [Business]. We're closed for Boxing Day and for the bank holiday on Monday 28 December. We're back on Tuesday 29 December, open 9am to 1pm until New Year's Eve. Please leave your name and number and we'll ring you back on Tuesday morning."

The week between Christmas and New Year

It plays when you're open but nobody picks up.

"Thanks for calling [Business]. We're running a small team between Christmas and New Year, and everyone's on another call. Leave your name, number and what it's about. Call before 2pm and we'll ring you back today; after that, tomorrow morning. Normal hours start again on Monday, January 4."

New Year's Eve voicemail message

"You've reached [Business] on New Year's Eve. The office closed at noon and stays closed for New Year's Day. Leave a message, or email [address], and we'll reply on Monday, January 4. Thank you for your business this year, and happy New Year."

New Year's Day voicemail greeting

"Happy New Year from [Business]. We're closed today, Friday, January 1, and open again on Monday, January 4, at 9am. Leave your name and number. If you're calling about new work, tell us a little about it so the right person calls you back."

A Scottish office says Tuesday 5 January, since Monday 4 is a bank holiday there.

Christmas emergency and on-call voicemail greeting

If a broken boiler or a server outage can't wait until January, the greeting has to get the caller to a person. We'd put the call through, since a panicking caller won't write down a number. The catch is that your on-call engineer also gets callers who only wanted your hours, so say who the line is for. For the other nights of the year, see After-hours answering for small businesses.

"Thanks for calling [Business]. We're closed for Christmas and back on Monday, January 4. If anyone is in danger, hang up and call the emergency services. If your call is urgent, stay on the line and we'll put you through to the engineer on call. If they can't pick up, leave a message and they'll call you back as soon as they can."

Christmas out of office phone message

A Christmas out of office phone message is the greeting on your own line while you're away: your name, the day you're back and who can help meanwhile. Use the same dates as your email auto reply.

Professional out of office message

"Hello, you've reached Priya Shah in accounts at [Business]. I'm out of the office for Christmas from Wednesday, December 23, and back on Monday, January 4. For an invoice or payment before then, call Tom on [number] or email accounts@[business].com. Otherwise, leave a message and I'll call you back that week."

Short out of office message (UK)

"Hi, it's Jo at [Business]. I'm off for Christmas and back in the office on Monday 4 January. If it can't wait, ring the main office on [number]. Someone's there from Tuesday 29 December. Have a lovely Christmas."

How to play your Christmas greeting automatically in CallForges

After the last date, calls take your usual route again. Owners and admins build and publish workflows.

  1. Open Workflows and the workflow your number uses.
  2. Put a Date rule straight after Incoming call, with your Time rule on its Business days path.
  3. Click New special date. Name it, pick your office's time zone and add your closures. A range can be whole days or set hours, so Christmas Eve can start at noon, and one special date can hold several ranges.

Each Date rule holds one special date, so a second script needs a second rule on the first one's Business days path, with its own special date and Audio message. The week-between greeting plays only when nobody answers, so its Date rule goes on the Unanswered path of your Ring users or teams step.
