What makes a good power dialer for a small team

A power dialer calls one lead at a time, and the next one rings when you hang up. A parallel dialer rings several at once, puts you through to the first person who answers and hangs up on the rest. A predictive dialer paces calls across a floor of call-center agents. What is a power dialer? and Power dialer vs parallel dialer explain each in full.

For a rep or a team of 3 to 10, we judged each option on five things:

  • The plan that holds the dialer, which some vendors put a tier or two above their entry price.
  • The seat minimum. Three reps on a nine-seat minimum pay for six empty seats.
  • One line or several, for the cold lists that rarely answer.
  • Voicemail drop and CRM logging, so reps don't read the same message all day or type up each call.
  • A price on the page, before any sales call.

How to choose a power dialer for your team

Start with dials per rep per day. Under about 50, a power dialer is enough, and everyone who picks up reaches a rep. A parallel dialer pays off on lists of hundreds where most calls ring out. Say a rep works 300 cold numbers and one in ten answers: on one line they sit through 270 misses in a row, and on two lines the misses run side by side. The price is the leads it cuts, since each pickup hangs up the other lines. We'd start at two lines and keep consumers and warm leads on the power dialer.

  • A solo rep or a pair: skip any seat minimum above your headcount. CallForges and CloudTalk Starter start at one seat; JustCall needs two, Aircall three.
  • Three to eight reps on cold B2B lists: get both dialers in one tool, so a dead list can move to two lines. CallForges has both on every plan, where Kixie needs its Multi-Line plan and CloudTalk a €39 add-on.
  • Nine or more SDRs: CallForges Scale, at $45 per user a month billed annually, adds custom retry rules for dialer campaigns, advanced analytics and wallboards, with the price on the page and no seat minimum.
  • Texting as much as calling: CallForges has two-way SMS and a shared team inbox on every plan, billed per message.

Setting up a power dialer in CallForges

Campaigns start with a paid plan, and during the 14-day free trial the Campaigns page reads Campaigns start with your plan. Use the trial, which needs no card, to set up your free local number and make first calls on its 50 inbound and 50 outbound minutes. Then, on a plan:

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Power Dialer, or Parallel Dialer and set Number of parallel calls. You can't change the type later.
  3. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see, and set Cooldown duration between two calls.
  4. Under Audience, click Select a list, or Upload leads to import a CSV.
  5. Turn on Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists and Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours, then set the hours.
  6. Click Create Campaign. It opens as a Draft; click Start Campaign when you're ready.

After each call, click the countdown to Skip Cooldown, or Log outcome to make it wait for your notes. On a parallel campaign, Re-dial cancelled leads starts at Never, so a lead the dialer cuts isn't called again (Create a campaign).

Common questions

What is the best power dialer?

For a small cold-calling team, CallForges. Both dialers and voicemail drop come on every plan, there's no seat minimum, and three reps pay $45 a month on Starter billed annually, under a third of JustCall Pro or Aircall Professional.

How many calls an hour can a power dialer make?

It depends on your talk time and your notes. As an example, if a miss takes 30 seconds from first ring to voicemail and your cooldown is 10 seconds, a rep who reaches nobody makes about 90 dials an hour. Each conversation takes minutes out of that, so a good hour shows fewer dials. How to make more calls per hour covers the rest.

What's the difference between a power dialer and a predictive dialer?

A predictive dialer dials ahead of your agents, betting one will be free when someone answers, so now and then a person picks up to silence. Call centers use it to keep many agents busy on one campaign. A power dialer waits for you, so whoever answers reaches a rep. Under ten reps, we'd stay with a power or parallel dialer.

For a small team, we think CallForges: it logs your calls in HubSpot automatically, on the Business and Scale plans (HubSpot integration). Nooks also says it integrates with HubSpot (Nooks pricing). Make a test call first and check what lands on the contact's timeline.
