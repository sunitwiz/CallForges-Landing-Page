Why contractors miss the calls they'd win

The calls go unanswered because the work needs both hands. You're on a pitch in a harness, in a crawl space with no signal, or driving between jobs with a ladder rack rattling overhead. Nobody should be digging a phone out of a pocket on a roof: falls are the leading cause of death in construction, and 389 of the 1,034 construction deaths in 2024 were falls to a lower level (OSHA, citing BLS data for 2024).

The homeowner on the other end isn't waiting for you. Water through a kitchen ceiling or a dead furnace in January means they call three companies in a row and book whoever gets back first, and your 5pm voicemail reaches someone who booked at 2.

A solo plumber has no office to catch those calls, and a roofing crew's office manager is often on the other line. We'd turn the text back on for every number a homeowner might ring, the one on the truck and the one on your website. The cost is the replies: someone has to answer them the same day, or the text turns into a promise you broke.

What to text back, by trade

The first text should collect what you'd ask on the phone, so you can tell an emergency from a quote while you're still on the job. Ask for a photo too: 91% of US adults own a smartphone (Pew Research Center, 2026), and one picture of a ceiling stain tells you more than a paragraph.

TradeThe call you missedWhat the text asks for
PlumberA leak, a backed-up drain, no hot waterThe address, what's leaking and whether they've shut the water off, plus a photo
ElectricianPower out in part of the house, a breaker that keeps tripping, a quote for a panelThe address, which rooms are out, plus a photo of the panel
HVACNo heat or no cooling, a noisy unit, a tune-upThe address, what the system is doing, plus a photo of the thermostat or the unit
RooferA leak after rain, hail or wind damage, a quoteThe address, where the water comes in, plus photos of the ceiling or of the roof taken from the ground

Three we'd start from, each under 160 characters (one text), with your business name in the brackets:

  • "Hi, it's Mike at [Business]. I'm on a job and missed your call. Text your address and what's leaking, plus a photo if you can. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • "Thanks for calling [Business]. Our techs are out. Reply with your address and what your system is doing and we'll call you back. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • "Hi, it's [Business], sorry we missed you. Text your address and photos of the damage taken from the ground. We'll call tonight. Reply STOP to opt out."

When to send it: daytime, after hours and storm weeks

In the day, send it within the first minute, while the homeowner is still scrolling the list. If your office often calls back within a couple of minutes, hold the text a minute or two so nobody gets your text and your call at once.

A real emergency shouldn't wait for a text. Send a burst pipe or a house with no heat to the on-call tech's cell first, and text only if nobody answers there either. After hours, the text should say when you'll call ("first thing tomorrow"), and an AI receptionist can take the calls where someone needs to talk tonight.

After a hailstorm, "we'll call you today" is a promise you'll break forty times. Switch the text to "we're booking inspections in order, usually within two days" and keep it true. Every caller then answers the same questions, so you can sort the replies by street and by how bad the photos look.

Rules for contractor texts

Two of these rules are laws, and registration is carrier policy.

RuleWho it applies toWhat it says (November 2026)
Revoking consent under the TCPA (FCC Report and Order 24-24, 2024)Businesses sending autodialed textsPeople can revoke consent in any reasonable way. A reply of "stop", "quit", "end", "revoke", "opt out", "cancel" or "unsubscribe" counts, and the sender must honor it within a reasonable time, 10 business days at most.

Stat. 501.059, 2024)Businesses sending sales calls or texts covered by the Florida actA text sent to sell consumer goods or services is a "telephonic sales call". We treat a text about the call a homeowner just made as a reply, and we'd keep it that way: no financing offer, no "ask about our gutter special". Rules can vary by state, so check your own. The full answer is in Is missed call text back legal?
