How much does an auto dialer cost?

You're pricing a dialer for five reps, and the three quotes that come back won't line up: one is per seat, one per minute, one per line that rings at once. An auto dialer costs what one real month costs your team, a seat for each rep plus the minutes and numbers they use. On CallForges, the power and parallel dialers come with every plan, from $15 per user a month billed annually ($19 billed monthly).

Four ways vendors price a dialer

Your three quotes each charge for a different unit, so the cheapest one depends on how much your team dials. A quote that wins for a team on the phones two hours a week can lose for a team that dials all day.

Per seat is the easiest to read: you pay for each rep, by the month or the year. Ask what the seat includes, because it might come with minutes and a number, or it might be a login with the calls on a separate bill. A team that dials less may do better per minute, paying for talk time at a rate for each country, so a quiet month costs less.

If you want a parallel dialer, watch for per-line pricing. A parallel dialer earns its keep by ringing several leads at once, and a per-line price makes you pay for each extra line. Flat-rate plans, which some vendors call "unlimited", look like the safe pick until a rep hits the vendor's fair use limit in the last week of the quarter, so ask where that limit sits.

For a team of five that dials every day, we'd take per seat with minutes included. You know the bill before the month starts, though you pay the same for a rep who makes 20 dials as for one who makes 200.

The dialer type can change the price too. We wouldn't put a team of five on a predictive dialer, the kind large call centers buy. It dials ahead of your reps and drops people who answer when no rep is free. US law limits that: under the FCC's rule, 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(7), a sales campaign may leave at most 3% of answered calls without a rep inside two seconds, measured over 30 days. If you're still choosing a type, start with What is an auto dialer and how does it work?, and Power dialer vs predictive dialer covers which one fits a small team.

This is general information, not legal advice.

What drives the bill after the seat price?

Start with the minutes. The carrier charges for each connected minute, and the dialer company passes that cost on to you, inside the seat or on top of it. Ask two things: how they round a minute, and whether a voicemail counts.

Say a rep works a 150-dial call block and 40 calls reach a person or a voicemail. On CallForges that's at least 40 minutes, even if half of them were 10-second voicemails, and the 110 calls that rang out or got a busy signal cost nothing. Under our Fair Usage Policy, we round any part of a minute up and count each connected call, voicemail included, as at least 1 minute.

Numbers are the next line on the bill. Ask how many come with a seat and what an extra one costs, because a rep calling into several regions may want more than one. Each CallForges user gets one free local number, and extra numbers start at $5 a month.

Then list the features your team uses today, say recording and CRM logging, and ask which ones cost extra, so you can add them to the seat price.

Ask who pays to register your caller ID as well. Fewer people pick up when a network labels your number "Spam Likely", and you shouldn't need a surprise fee to fix that. We include verified caller ID, where the carrier signs your calls to vouch for the number, and your company name as caller ID (CNAM) for US numbers on every plan.

Last, the contract: ask about seat minimums and how you cancel, since a minimum of, say, ten seats doubles the bill for five reps. We have no seat minimum, and monthly plans cancel anytime.
