What makes a good AI voice agent for a small business

We judged the list on five things:

  • The build. A ready-made agent works once you add your information and pick a number; a platform hands a developer the parts.
  • It answers only from your information and says when it doesn't know. A language model can quote a wrong price in a calm voice.
  • It tells callers it's an automated assistant, and a caller who wants a person can reach one.
  • The hand-off: a transfer to your team, a callback or a booked appointment.
  • A bill you can predict, with plain rules on which minutes or calls count.

How to choose an AI voice agent

Start with the calls you miss. Say 60 calls a month come in after hours, at 3 minutes each: 180 minutes. On CallForges Starter that's $21.60 of agent time, and on Business a three-person team's 300 pooled minutes cover it. Rosie's $49 plan covers it too. Retell AI charges $12.60 to $55.80 for the same minutes plus $2 for the number, and someone still has to build the agent. For two calls a night, we wouldn't hand a small office a developer platform. Then ask each option:

  • Who builds it? CallForges is ready-made; Retell AI, Vapi and Bland AI need someone to build the agent.
  • What happens when it can't answer? The CallForges agent books a callback for your team instead of guessing.
  • Can a caller reach a person? CallForges hands the caller to your team when they ask, and agent minutes stop there.
  • Does it help you make calls too? Every CallForges plan has the power and parallel dialers alongside the agent.

For sales teams, we'd cover inbound first, so the lead who calls back at 8pm gets a callback booked instead of a voicemail. If you want AI placing calls, CallForges has an outbound agent that calls people for you, alongside the power and parallel dialers. AI calls come with consent rules, and What is an AI voice agent? covers them.
