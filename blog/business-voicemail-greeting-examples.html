Business voicemail greeting examples: 12 professional scripts

A business voicemail greeting tells callers who they've reached and when they'll hear back, then asks for the one detail you need, in under 30 seconds. Below are 12 professional examples for everyday calls, from after hours to a sales line, and how to set each one in CallForges. Say a new customer rings a plumbing firm at 12:40 on a Tuesday. Everyone's at lunch, and the line says "Please leave a message after the tone", with no name and no callback time, so they ring the next plumber on their list.

Key takeaways
  • Say your business name first and your callback time second: callers listen for those two.
  • Give each situation its own greeting. Someone calling at 9pm doesn't need your lunch hour.
  • Promise a callback window your team can keep, and let the phone system pick the greeting by time of day.

What should a professional voicemail greeting say?

It names your business and says why nobody picked up and when the caller will hear back. Then it asks for one useful detail and stops. Every script below fills in this template:

"Thanks for calling [Business]. [Why nobody answered.] Leave your name, number and [the one detail you need], and [who] will call you back [when]. [Optional: a faster route.]"

The callback time does the most work. "As soon as possible" tells a caller nothing, so they keep dialing; "within the hour" gives them a reason to wait, as long as you keep it. The detail should save a second call: the job address for a tradesperson, an order number for a support desk. Cut the music before the beep and the line about how important their call is.

Your own recorded voice tells the caller a person will hear the message. A typed greeting read out by text to speech is quicker to change when your hours change. We'd record the main greeting and type the ones that change, like a lunch-hour message.

Business voicemail greeting examples for everyday calls

Swap in your name, hours and callback window. Each runs well under 30 seconds.

Standard professional greeting (during business hours)

"Thanks for calling [Business]. We're with other clients right now. Leave your name, your number and a line about what you need, and we'll call you back within two business hours."

Small business voicemail greeting

With one or two people, use your first name, so the caller knows who'll hear the message.

"Hi, you've reached [Business], this is [Name]. I'm on a job and can't get to the phone. Leave your name, number and the address of the job, and I'll call you back before the end of the day."

Business voicemail greeting with hours

Use this one if a single greeting plays day and night.

"Thank you for calling [Business]. We're open Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm, and Saturday mornings until noon. Leave your name and number. If we're open, we'll ring you back within the hour; if not, first thing the next morning we're in."

After-hours voicemail greeting

"You've reached [Business]. We're closed for the day and open again at 8am, Monday to Friday. Leave your name, number and what you need, and we'll return messages from 8, in the order they came in. Order updates are on [website] any time."

Lunch break voicemail greeting

"Hi, you've reached [Business]. We're at lunch until 1pm. Leave your name and number and we'll call you back this afternoon, or try us again after 1."

Busy line: everyone's on another call

This plays when your team is in the office but every line is taken.

"Thanks for hanging on, you've reached [Business]. We're all on other calls right now. Leave your name, number and the reason for your call, and the first person free will call you back, usually within 30 minutes."

Greetings for sales, support and a business cell phone

A sales or support line asks for different details, and a rep's own line should sound like a person.

Sales team voicemail greeting

On a sales line, the callback promise is your pitch. We'd say "today" and keep it.

"You've reached the sales team at [Business]. We're all on calls with customers. Leave your name, your company and the best time to reach you, and one of us will call you back today. Comparing quotes? Say so, and we'll get yours out first."

Sales rep's personal voicemail greeting

"Hi, it's [Name] at [Business]. I'm with a customer right now. Leave your name and number and I'll call you back within the hour. If it's about an order you've already placed, [colleague] on [number] can help straight away."

Customer service voicemail greeting

"Thanks for calling [Business] support. Everyone's helping other customers. Leave your name, number and order number, and tell us in a sentence what's wrong. We call back in the order messages arrive, within one business day. Many answers are on [help page] too."

Business voicemail greeting for a cell phone

When your business number rings on your mobile through an app, callers hear this greeting and your personal voicemail stays personal.

"Hi, you've reached [Name] with [Business]. I'm driving or on site, so I can't pick up. Leave your name, number and what you need, and I'll call you back today. For a quote, you can also email [address]."

Out of office voicemail greeting (not a holiday)

"Hi, it's [Name] at [Business]. I'm at a trade show until Thursday the 12th and checking messages each evening. Leave your name and number and I'll call you back the next morning. If it's urgent, [colleague] on [number] can help."

Voicemail greeting in Spanish

Play English first, then Spanish, so both groups of callers hear their language before the beep.

"Thanks for calling [Business]. Leave your name, number and a short message, and we'll call you back today. Gracias por llamar a [Business]. Deje su nombre, su número de teléfono y un breve mensaje, y le devolveremos la llamada hoy."

Business voicemail greeting FAQ

How long should a business voicemail greeting be?

Under 30 seconds, and shorter is better. The caller is waiting for the beep, and each extra sentence is one more chance for them to hang up. Time it, and cut anything that isn't your name, your callback window or the detail you need.

What's a good short voicemail greeting for business?

One sentence can do it: "Thanks for calling [Business]; leave your name and number and we'll call you back today." It works for a busy line or a one-person business, as long as "today" stays true.
