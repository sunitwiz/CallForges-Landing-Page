What makes a good business phone system for a small business

A business phone system (VoIP, or a virtual phone system) carries calls over the internet, with your numbers in apps instead of a box in a cupboard. We judged each option on six things:

  • Numbers in your countries, and porting, so customers keep calling the number on your van.
  • Routing: hours and holidays, a short phone menu, and a team that rings together or in turn.
  • Texting from the business number, including US registration (10DLC) and its fees.
  • Desktop, web and mobile apps, so nobody needs a handset.
  • AI notes after each call, and whether they cost extra.
  • The price for your headcount on the plan you'd use, billed the way you'll pay.

The 7 best business phone systems for small business

We make CallForges, so it's first on this list. Every other price below comes from that company's own pricing page, checked 25 November 2026.

CallForges: a phone system with dialers built in

CallForges runs your business numbers in desktop, web, iOS and Android apps, with no hardware. Each user gets a free local US, UK, Canada or Australia number in a minute, and numbers come in 56 countries and territories. Call workflows handle your hours and holidays, and night calls can go to the AI voice agent. Plans start at $15 per user a month billed annually ($19 monthly), with no seat minimum and both the power and parallel dialers (CallForges pricing). Calling isn't unlimited, though. Starter includes 100 free minutes per user a month for calls to and from those four countries, and the phone menu starts on Business, at $29. We built it for a team that answers the phone and dials out every day.

RingCentral: phones and meetings across sites

RingCentral's RingEX Core costs $20 per user a month billed annually ($30 monthly), with unlimited domestic calling and 25 texts per user a month. Advanced, at $25, adds CRM integrations and raises texts to 100 (RingCentral pricing, checked November 2026). The page warns that regulatory fees and taxes may be added, RingEX lists no dialer, and renting desk phones takes a multi-year contract. It suits a business replacing an office phone system across sites that wants phones and meetings from one vendor.

Dialpad: AI notes on the cheapest plan

Dialpad Connect Standard costs $15 per user a month billed annually or $27 monthly, and puts calls, meetings and messaging in one app. That base price includes real-time transcripts and instant call summaries, which Dialpad calls AI Recaps (Dialpad pricing, checked November 2026). No Connect plan lists a dialer, and the page says your price may differ by region and currency. For AI notes and meetings next to your phone at $15, it's a strong pick.

Nextiva: phones and live chat from one company

Nextiva's Go plan, at $10 per user a month, covers calls and texts and includes video and team chat. Business, at $25, adds call routing and live chat, and supports desk phones (Nextiva pricing, checked November 2026). The page says monthly billing costs more without showing the figure, and there's no free trial. It fits a team whose customers also use live chat.

Quo (formerly OpenPhone): calls and texts in the US and Canada

Quo starts at $15 per user a month billed annually ($19 monthly) on Starter, with unlimited calls and texts in the US and Canada under fair usage. Business, at $23 ($33 monthly), adds phone menus and AI summaries for every call. Porting is free (Quo pricing, checked November 2026). No plan lists a dialer, calls outside the US and Canada cost extra, and the trial lasts 7 days. For a small US team that mostly answers and texts, we'd put it near the top.

Google Voice: a number for Google Workspace teams

For a team already paying for Google Workspace, Google Voice Starter adds a business number for $10 per user a month, for up to 10 users, but without an auto attendant (a phone menu) or ring groups. Standard, at $20, adds both, plus call queuing and AI note-taking (Google Voice plans, checked November 2026). You pay for Workspace on top, and numbers outside your home country need Premier, at $30. Pick it if your small team already lives in Gmail.

Aircall: for support teams of three or more

Aircall charges $30 per license a month for Essentials, billed annually, with call and voicemail transcription and up to 4,000 outbound texts per user a month. Professional, at $50, adds a Power Dialer and AI call summaries (Aircall pricing, checked November 2026). Most plans need at least 3 licenses, and licenses paid annually upfront can't be reduced mid-term (Aircall help center). It fits support teams that work inside a help desk all day.

How to choose a phone system for your team

Start with what your team does on the phone all day.

  • Answering, and texting US or Canadian customers: Quo, with unlimited calls and texts there. A five-person office that never dials out may be happier on Quo than on our Starter plan and its 100 free minutes per user.
  • Answering and dialing out every day: CallForges. Its parallel dialer, on every plan, rings several contacts at once and connects you to the first who answers.
  • Video meetings in the same app as your calls: Dialpad or RingCentral.
  • Keeping desk phones: Nextiva Business or Google Voice. CallForges runs in apps only.
  • Customers in the UK or Australia: CallForges, whose free minutes cover calls to and from both.

Then price it at your headcount. Say eight people on the plan with a phone menu: $160 a month on Google Voice Standard plus Workspace, then $184 on Quo Business and $232 on CallForges Business, both billed annually. Ours costs the most of the three, and the difference buys both dialers, plus free minutes that also cover the UK and Australia. Without a menu, eight people on CallForges Starter pay $120.

Setting up your business phone system in CallForges

The first two steps fit inside the free trial.

  1. Numbers. Open Phone Numbers, click Add Number, pick a Country and Number type, click Save & Next, then Confirm & Purchase for your free number. To keep yours, click Port number instead: $10 per US number, charged once the port date is confirmed, and your old system works until the move completes (Port your number to CallForges).
  2. Routing. Open Workflows, click New workflow, pick Business hours + voicemail and click Use template. Set the Time rule to your hours, add a Date rule for holidays and choose a Ringing order, such as Ring all or Ring in round robin. Click Publish, then Numbers and Save numbers (Build a call workflow).
  3. Texting. UK and Australian mobile numbers text with no registration. For US numbers, get your Business profile approved in the Compliance center, then click Add campaign under Text messaging (A2P 10DLC). Registering starts with your plan, and the carriers usually take 1 to 3 weeks.

Night calls can go to the AI voice agent or an on-call mobile instead, and After-hours answering for small businesses shows both. The free trial runs 14 days with no card, a free local number, and 50 inbound and 50 outbound minutes. Calls stop at 5 minutes until you add a card. Start your free trial.

Questions about small business phone systems

What is the best phone system for a small business in the UK, Canada or Australia?

Check each vendor's local pricing page first. CallForges gives you UK, Canadian and Australian numbers in a minute, each user's free local number can be in any of the three, and every plan's free minutes cover calls to and from them. We bill in US dollars: extra local numbers start at $5 a month, and toll-free at $5, or $25 in Australia (CallForges pricing).

Which business phone system is best for texting?

In the US, the carriers set the first step for every provider: you register your business and a campaign (A2P 10DLC) before local numbers can text. Quo lists $19.50 once and $1.50 to $3 a month, then unlimited US and Canada texts (Quo pricing). On CallForges, the carriers' fees come to 19.5 credits for a first campaign ($1 buys 1 credit) and 10 a month once approved, plus each text, and the team shares one inbox. For heavy US texting alone, Quo should cost you less. For texting plus dialing, we'd pick CallForges.
