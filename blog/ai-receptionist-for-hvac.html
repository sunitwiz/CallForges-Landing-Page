Where HVAC companies lose calls

An HVAC phone line has two problems most offices don't. Demand comes in waves, so the first heat wave or cold snap brings a week of calls at once. And many calls arrive when no one's at a desk: evenings, weekends, or mid-afternoon when the owner is in an attic.

A homeowner with no cooling in July doesn't leave a voicemail and wait. They call the next company on the list. The trade press covers this as missed calls turning into lost jobs, and owners trying voice agents to catch them (ACHR News).

Count yours before you buy anything. Pull last month's call history and look for calls that rang out or went to voicemail, and when they came in. If most are after 5pm and on weekends, that's where an AI receptionist earns its keep.

What to give it before peak season

Write these down in plain sentences, one topic each, before the first heat wave:

  • Your hours, holidays and the ZIP codes or towns you cover.
  • The fees you publish: service call, after-hours call, tune-up, and what a maintenance plan includes.
  • The brands and system types you work on, and the ones you don't.
  • What to tell someone with no heat or no cooling, and what to tell someone who smells gas.

Then call your own number and ask the ten questions your office hears most, plus two you never wrote down, and listen to what it says.

It answers only from what you give it. Say you raised your after-hours service fee in October and never updated the FAQ: callers that night hear the old fee. Update the documents the same day you change a price.

Emergencies: who should answer at night?

We'd keep a person on real emergencies. Someone with no heat and a baby in the house wants to hear a human say a tech is coming. And a gas smell isn't an HVAC call at all; it's a call to the gas company or 911, and your greeting should say so.

The setup that works for most shops is a short phone menu. "Press 1 if you have no heat or no cooling" goes to the on-call tech's mobile. Everything else goes to the AI receptionist, which answers questions and books a callback for the morning. The tech's phone only rings for the calls that justify it.

Rules for HVAC calls and texts

Answering calls has few special rules. The ones that matter are about calls and texts you send.

  • Outbound AI calls. The FCC confirmed in 2024 that AI-generated voices count as "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the TCPA, so AI calls to residential lines and mobiles need the person's prior express consent unless an exemption applies (FCC Declaratory Ruling, 2024). That's a law.
  • Texts from a US number, like "your tech is on the way", need the number registered with the carriers first (A2P 10DLC). That's carrier policy. Our guide to 10DLC registration explains it.

This is general information, not legal advice.
