What is CNAM? Show your business name on outgoing calls

CNAM (caller ID name) is the business name shown with your number when you call, such as ACME INC. Your call carries only your number: the called person's carrier looks the name up in a caller name database. To show your name, you register it so it is in those databases.

What is CNAM?

CNAM (Caller ID Name, also called calling name) is the name a phone shows next to the number of an incoming call. For a business, it is the line that says ACME INC instead of only (512) 555-0142. Without one, the person you call sees your number and usually its city and state.

The name is what people read before they decide to pick up. A number they don't know tells them nothing. A business name tells them who is calling.

How does your name reach the phone you call?

Your name is looked up at the far end of the call, not sent with it. The call carries your number. The carrier of the person you call takes that number, asks a caller name database which name belongs to it, and sends the answer to the phone.

The FCC put it plainly: the phone network's signaling does "not transmit the name of the calling party along with the number", so caller ID services find the name "by sending a query to a centralized calling name (CNAM) database" (FCC Truth in Caller ID order, 2011). There is no single database. Some carriers run their own, and others buy the lookup from companies that gather listings from many sources, often through "a chain of feeds through several layers of providers and resellers" (FCC report to Congress on caller ID, 2011).

This has two results worth knowing. Registering a name means getting it into those databases, which your carrier does for you. And because the called person's carrier does the lookup, it also decides whether to show a name at all.

Why does your name show on some phones and not others?

Your name shows on landlines with caller ID, and on mobiles whose carrier shows caller names on that line. Caller name lookups began on landline phones (FCC, 2025). On some mobile networks, seeing the name of an unknown caller is a feature the person has to turn on, or pay for.

Verizon's help page about callers' names is a clear example. It says a caller's name appears on a Verizon mobile only when all of these are true, and that on other networks "it's the person's service provider (not Verizon) that displays a caller's name" (Verizon Share Name ID FAQs, checked 2026):

  • The phone works with Verizon's Call Filter app.
  • The phone has Call Filter Plus or another caller ID service turned on.
  • The person hasn't saved your number as a contact.

On Verizon, caller ID is one of the features of Call Filter Plus, at $3.99 a month (Verizon Call Filter FAQs, checked 2026). So when a rep says "my name didn't show on my own mobile", nothing is broken: their carrier may not show caller names on that line. Test on a landline, or on a phone with caller ID turned on.

Remember the last one too: a person who saved your number sees the name they saved it under, not yours.

What are the rules for a caller ID name?

A caller ID name holds at most 15 characters, and spaces count. Verizon's limit for its own customers' names, for example, is "up to 15 characters with at least 1 letter" (Verizon Share Name ID FAQs, checked 2026). Each provider sets its own list of allowed characters on top of that.

In CallForges a Display name follows these rules:

  • Up to 15 characters, spaces included. The field counts them as you type.
  • Letters, numbers, spaces, periods and commas only, so no &, hyphen or apostrophe.
  • It starts with a letter.
  • It can't be just the name of a US state, such as TEXAS.

Fifteen characters go fast, so plan the name before you register it:

Acme Roofing Company LLCACME ROOFING COExactly 15 with spaces, so LLC goes
Smith & Sons PlumbingSMITH AND SONSNo & allowed, so spell it out
Virelix Systems, sales teamVIRELIX SALESA second name for the sales numbers

Use the name people know you by. In CallForges a caller ID name needs a Business profile registered with an EIN or DUNS number, because the carriers check the name against US business records. So pick a name that matches your business.

How long does it take for CNAM to show?

Plan for two waits. In CallForges, the Business profile is approved in about 1 to 2 days, and once the carriers approve your caller ID name, they start showing it 48 to 72 hours later.

A new name does not appear everywhere at the same moment. Each network reads its own database, or buys the lookup from someone else (FCC, 2011), so a new name can show on one network before another. If the name shows on a landline but not on a mobile, check the mobile's caller ID setting before anything else.

How does CNAM fit with STIR/SHAKEN and spam labels?

CNAM shows who is calling, STIR/SHAKEN proves the number is really yours, and number reputation affects whether the person sees a spam warning. They do different jobs, and a spam label can replace your name.

What it doesWhat the person sees
CNAM (caller ID name)Puts your business name on your number in the caller name databasesACME INC instead of a bare number
STIR/SHAKENYour carrier signs the call, so the called network can check the number wasn't fakedUsually nothing directly. Their network uses the signature when it judges the call.
Number reputationRegisters your numbers and why you call with the networks' spam analyticsA lower risk of a spam warning in place of your name

STIR/SHAKEN is a set of standards that lets "authenticated information about a call" travel with it, and its level A says the signing carrier knows its customer and that customer's right to use the number (FCC, 2025). STIR/SHAKEN itself doesn't put your name on the screen. The FCC says "legacy CNAM databases currently remain the only widespread source of caller name information", and in October 2025 it proposed requiring carriers to pass on a verified caller name with calls whose number is unlikely to be spoofed (FCC, 2025). That is a proposal, not a rule.

A registered name does not protect you from a spam label. On AT&T's business phone service, a call judged to be spam is "delivered with Suspected Spam as the caller ID name" (AT&T Call Protect, checked 2026). So a team that calls a lot registers all three: the name, the signature and the reputation.
