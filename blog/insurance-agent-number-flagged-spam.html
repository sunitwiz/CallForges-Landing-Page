Insurance agents: why your number gets flagged as spam

It's AEP, Medicare's annual enrollment season, and you're working aged final expense leads between appointments: a few hundred dials a day from one number, double dialing anyone who doesn't pick up. Then your pickup rate tanks and a client says your call came up "Scam Likely". The called person's network or a phone app scored your number by how it calls, and yours looked like a robocaller's. Change the pattern, then register the number.

Why do insurance agents' numbers get flagged as spam?

The scoring counts bursts of dials against you, and short unanswered calls and spam reports too. No law sets these labels. They're carrier policy: each network or app scores its own way and changes it without notice.

You start with a handicap in insurance, because robocallers pitch it too. The FCC counted about 23.6 million health insurance robocalls a day crossing the four largest US wireless networks (FCC, 2020) when it proposed the biggest fine in its history, against a health insurance telemarketer behind about 1 billion spoofed robocalls in under five months. It imposed the $225 million fine the next year (FCC Forfeiture Order, 2021). You ring the same phones about the same products, so your calling has to look different.

On insurance-forums.com, agents post "Scam likely?" and ask for help with a number getting flagged as spam.

Aged leads, double dials and AEP

Carriers disconnect about 35 million US numbers a year and make them available to new owners (FCC Second Report and Order, 2018), so part of a year-old list rings strangers who never asked for a quote. They decline, or they tap report.

We'd drop the double dial first. Agents do it because some leads grab the second ring, but say you double dial 200 leads in an afternoon: that's up to 400 calls from one number, half to people who let the first ring out. You lose a few late pickups and keep a number clients still answer.

In AEP you take a number that sat quiet all summer to full volume overnight. Medicare.gov calls it the Open Enrollment Period, October 15 to December 7 (Medicare.gov, checked 2026). Ramp the number up in the weeks before.

In P&C, X-date calls reach prospects near the X-date, when their current policy expires. From your main line, any label they earn shows on your claim calls too, so keep them off it.

Why rotating or spoofing numbers backfires

Sooner or later someone on the floor says to buy a stack of new numbers and spread the dials. We wouldn't. A fresh number calling the same list the same way ends up with the same label, because the scoring follows how you call. Rest the number while you fix the pattern, then bring it back at a human pace.

The rules still apply to your agency. Florida counts calls "regardless of the phone number used", and under the FCC's rule a "don't call me" applies to the agency, so it covers every number you have. Leads also call back the number they saw, and a dropped number can't take their call or their do-not-call request.

Showing someone else's number is worse. It can't get level A, the top level of SHAKEN/STIR (how US carriers vouch for caller ID), which needs the carrier to know your business and its right to the number. And its owner gets your callbacks: in the FCC's $225 million case, the companies whose numbers the telemarketer spoofed were "overwhelmed with angry call-backs" (FCC, 2020).

What to do when your number is flagged

Review your calling habits before you dispute a label, as USTelecom, a telecom industry group, recommends in its best practices for redress requests (2026). That's guidance; no law requires it.

  1. Confirm the label by calling phones on a few networks from the number, then check what changed in the last two weeks, like a new aged-lead list or more dials per number.
  2. Scrub the list against the national Do Not Call Registry and your own list, and drop numbers that changed hands: the FCC's reassigned numbers database shows whether a number was permanently disconnected since you got consent (47 CFR 64.1200(m)).
  3. Make fewer dials a day and let each call ring out instead of double dialing.
  4. Register the business and the number, then dispute the label with the network that shows it (how to check and dispute a label).
