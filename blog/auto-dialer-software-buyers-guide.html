Does it have the dialer your lists need?

A power dialer calls one lead at a time, and the next one rings when you hang up. A parallel dialer rings several leads at once and puts you through to the first who answers. A predictive dialer paces calls across a floor of agents, which a team of five doesn't need.

Our rule of thumb: warm leads, callbacks and renewals go on a power dialer, because everyone who picks up reaches a rep. Big cold lists, where most calls ring out, go on a parallel dialer. The trade-off is that once someone answers, the dialer hangs up on the other lines, so some leads hear a ring and then nothing. We'd start at two lines. Power dialer vs parallel dialer goes deeper.

Check which plan holds each mode, because some vendors keep multi-line dialing a tier or two up. In CallForges both come with every plan. You choose the dialer for each campaign, and on a parallel campaign you set how many lines ring at once, so a dead list can move to two lines while warm leads stay on one.

Voicemail drop belongs on the same list. You record your message once, and when a machine answers, the dialer leaves it for you instead of a rep reading the same message all afternoon. Every CallForges plan has it. On a parallel campaign it plays by itself when machine detection is on, and the lead is marked as a dropped voicemail. In power mode it's one click.

Who it calls, when, and how often

A dialer that rings at 7am or calls someone who asked you to stop costs you more than the minutes. Look for three controls: calling hours in the lead's own time zone, a do-not-call list it obeys, and a way to call missed leads again.

A CallForges campaign takes the days and hours you allow, and calls each lead only inside them in the lead's own time zone; a US number uses its area code's. A lead whose turn comes outside those hours is skipped for that campaign, so start your call block while your leads are at their desks. Turn on the do-not-call option and nobody on your Do Not Call list is dialed.

For retries, a parallel campaign can send a lead the dialer cut back to the bottom of the list, as many times as you set. On any campaign, you can tick the leads who didn't answer and create a follow-up campaign from them. Scale adds custom retry rules for dialer campaigns.

Two US rules sit behind these settings. The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule bars telemarketing calls to a person's home outside 8am to 9pm at the called person's location, unless they've agreed (16 CFR 310.4(c), eCFR, 2026). The same section bars calls to people who've told the seller not to call, and to numbers on the National Do Not Call Registry unless the seller has the person's written agreement or an existing business relationship. Your own list and the national registry are separate checks: scrub the list against the registry before you load it. Both rules are about sales calls to consumers, and the rule exempts most business-to-business calls (16 CFR 310.6, eCFR, 2026). What protects your numbers?

Once a number shows "Spam Likely", the list stops answering, whatever dialer you use. Ask the vendor whether it can call from a number local to the lead, register your caller ID with the carriers and warn you when a number starts getting ignored.

On Business and Scale, CallForges' local caller ID calls each lead from a number in their own country, so a lead in Leeds sees a UK number. Every user gets one free local US, UK, Canada or Australia number, and extra numbers in 56 countries and territories start at $5 a month.

In the Compliance center, one business profile moves your US calls from SHAKEN/STIR level B to level A, the carriers' check that your caller ID is real. From there you can register a caller ID name, which shows 48 to 72 hours after approval, and your numbers' reputation. All of it is free on every plan and during the trial. Then number health on the Dashboard marks a number "May be labelled spam" when its pickup rate drops, so you rest that number before the whole list goes quiet. Where does each call end up?

If reps type up every call by hand, your CRM falls behind by Wednesday. Ask the vendor to log a test call in front of you and show where it lands.
