How to set up an AI receptionist, step by step

It's 5:40 on a Friday. Your team has gone home, and the office line will ring a dozen times before Monday, each call ending in voicemail. Setting up an AI receptionist to take those calls is an afternoon's work. You list what callers ask, gather the sources it answers from and write a short greeting. Then you decide when it picks up and who gets the call when someone wants a person. In CallForges, it's one step in your number's call workflow.

CallForges team · 27 November 2026

Key takeaways
  • Most of the work comes before any settings: the questions your callers ask, and written answers the agent can use.

  • Give callers a clear way out: a greeting that says it's automated and how to reach a person, and someone who makes the callbacks.

Before you start: the questions callers ask, and your answers

An AI receptionist knows your business only as well as the material you give it, so spend the first hour with a notepad. (What is an AI receptionist? covers how one handles a call.)

Pull last month's missed calls and voicemails and write down every question you hear. For most small businesses the same ten or so come back, such as your hours, the areas you cover or whether an order is ready. Then sort them into two columns.

Give it to the agentSend it to a person
Opening hours, holidays includedA quote for a job nobody has seen
Service areas and what you doA complaint
Fixed fees you already publishAn emergency
Policies: cancellations, deposits, returnsAnything you haven't written down

The left column becomes the agent's sources. It can read help articles, pages on your website, files such as a PDF price list, or plain text you type in. Write each answer the way you'd say it on the phone. "Our callout fee is $89 and comes off the bill if you book the job" (an example) beats a paragraph of terms.

Keep each fact in one place. If your hours sit in three documents and you update one, callers may hear the old two. And name the person who updates the sources when a price or a policy changes. The right column tells you who should get the hand-off and the callbacks.

Write a short greeting

Callers decide in the first few seconds whether to stay on, so two sentences is plenty. The first says who they've reached and that they're talking to an automated assistant. The second says what it can help with and how to get a person. For example:

"Hi, you've reached Harbor Plumbing. I'm an automated assistant and can answer questions about our hours, service areas and callout fee. Say 'a person' at any time to reach our team."

Skip the list of everything it can do, and skip the jokes. A caller who hears a long speech before they can ask anything starts looking for the hang-up button. Callers hang up on AI receptionists covers what drives people off and what keeps them on.

Choose when it answers: after hours, overflow or all day

You decide which calls the agent gets with your call routing. Three setups are common.

SetupCalls it takesFits when
After hoursNights, weekends and holidaysThose calls go to voicemail today
OverflowDaytime calls your team doesn't pick up in timeCalls bunch up first thing or at lunch
All dayEvery call, firstMost calls are routine questions with a written answer

We'd start with after hours. A caller at 9pm would otherwise hear a beep, so each call the agent answers is one you'd have missed. A few weeks of real calls also show you which answers are missing. The catch with after hours alone is that a daytime call nobody picks up still ends in voicemail. Add overflow once you trust the answers. Give your team the first 20 to 25 seconds of ringing, then let the agent take it.

Treat holidays as nights. If the office is shut on Thanksgiving, those calls should take the same path as 2am. After-hours answering for small businesses compares the agent with the other ways to cover closed hours.

Decide who gets the hand-off and the callbacks

The agent passes a call on in two ways. A caller who asks for a person goes to whoever you chose, and a question your sources don't answer gets a callback instead of a guess.

For the hand-off, pick the team that handles that kind of call in your open hours, such as support or the front desk. At night, pick whoever is on call. If you take emergency calls after hours, make the on-call phone the hand-off target.

Callbacks only help if someone makes them. Name one person to work through them when you open, and agree how fast. We'd aim for the first hour of the day, while the caller still remembers why they rang.

Test it once your plan is on

Once your plan is on and the workflow is published, call your own number from your mobile at a time the agent answers. Work through a short script:

  1. Ask your ten most common questions, in the words your callers use.
  2. Ask two you never wrote down. It should offer to book a callback.
  3. Ask for a person, and check the call reaches the right phone.
  4. Have a colleague call in another language, or with a different accent. The agent speaks more than 30 languages.

Fix the sources wherever an answer came out wrong, then call again. Repeat the script whenever you change a price or your hours.
