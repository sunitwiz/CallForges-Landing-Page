What we mean by a reliable dialer

A reliable dialer is one whose calls get answered. Staying online is the least it owes you; the test happens on a stranger's phone, in the moment before they decide whether to pick up.

Three things decide whether your call is answered

Before anyone says hello, their phone has shown them three things about you: whether your caller ID is verified, the name next to your number, and whether their network has labeled you spam. You can do something about each one.

  • The signature. Under the FCC's STIR/SHAKEN rules, a US law, phone companies sign calls so the receiving network can check that the caller ID is real. At level A, the highest, the signing company vouches that its customer can use the number (FCC Eighth Report and Order, 2024). We explain it in What is STIR/SHAKEN?
  • The name. A bare number makes people guess, and a business name answers the question for them. That's caller ID name, or CNAM. It shows on landlines with caller ID and on mobiles whose carrier shows caller names, so it varies by carrier: What is CNAM?
  • The label. The network of the person you call, or an app on their phone, rates your number from how it calls, and a low rating shows "Spam Likely" where your name should be. The FCC describes these labels as "generally based on analytics" (FCC Seventh Report and Order, 2023). That's carrier policy, and each network sets its own: Phone number reputation.

You usually find out about all three the hard way, when a good list stops answering. We'd rather you set them up on day one.
