What makes a good AI voice agent for a small business

We judged the list on five things:

  • The build. A ready-made agent works once you add your information and pick a number; a platform hands a developer the parts.
  • It answers only from your information and says when it doesn't know. A language model can quote a wrong price in a calm voice.
  • It tells callers it's an automated assistant, and a caller who wants a person can reach one.
  • The hand-off: a transfer to your team, a callback or a booked appointment.
  • A bill you can predict, with plain rules on which minutes or calls count.

Vapi: each part at the provider's price

Vapi's $0 plan comes with $5 in credits and charges $0.05 a minute for hosting, and Core costs $29 a month, pre-paid and auto-renewing. On top of hosting, Vapi passes on each provider's cost, such as the language model and the voice, "with no markup" (Vapi pricing, checked November 2026). Bland AI: plans sized by calls a day

Synthflow: an annual enterprise contract

Synthflow's own pricing page now says every new deployment starts on an Enterprise contract: an annual agreement priced against the call volume you expect, with no rate by the minute and no free plan or trial (Synthflow pricing, checked November 2026). How to choose an AI voice agent

Start with the calls you miss. Say 60 calls a month come in after hours, at 3 minutes each: 180 minutes. On CallForges Starter that's $21.60 of agent time, and on Business a three-person team's 300 pooled minutes cover it. Rosie's $49 plan covers it too. Retell AI charges $12.60 to $55.80 for the same minutes plus $2 for the number, and someone still has to build the agent. For two calls a night, we wouldn't hand a small office a developer platform.

  • Up to 25 calls a month: Smith.ai's free plan, and we'd start there.
  • A small office that misses calls after hours and also makes them: CallForges, since the agent and both dialers come on every plan.
  • Callers who need an appointment booked during the call: Rosie or Smith.ai.
  • A developer and a job no ready-made agent covers: Retell AI or Vapi, or Bland AI if the job is placing calls.

For sales teams, we'd cover inbound first, so the lead who calls back at 8pm gets a callback booked instead of a voicemail. A rep making 40 calls a day doesn't need an AI dialing for them. If you want AI placing calls, CallForges has an outbound agent that calls people for you, alongside the power and parallel dialers. Setting up the CallForges AI agent for after-hours calls

The agent answers only from your docs, so write down what callers ask, such as your hours and prices, and give it those FAQs and policies. Then send it your after-hours calls with a call workflow:

  1. Open Workflows, click New workflow, pick Business hours + voicemail and click Use template.
  2. Set the Time rule to your own hours and time zone.
  3. Send At any other time to the AI agent, or to an IVR menu whose emergency key goes to a Forward call to the on-call mobile.
  4. Add a Date rule for each holiday, so it goes where your nights go.
  5. Click Save draft, then Publish. Click Numbers, pick your number and click Save numbers.

Questions about AI voice agents

Can an AI voice agent transfer calls to a person?

The ones that list it can, at different prices. The CallForges agent hands the call to your team when the caller asks, and agent minutes stop there; the rest of the call uses normal calling minutes. Bland AI bills transferred time at $0.05 a minute on Start and $0.04 on Build, and Rosie offers warm transfers from its $149 plan.

Can an AI voice agent book appointments in my calendar?

Some can. Rosie books into your calendar from its $149 plan and texts a booking link on the $49 plan, and Smith.ai lists appointment booking on its AI plans. The CallForges agent books a callback instead, and your team calls back.
