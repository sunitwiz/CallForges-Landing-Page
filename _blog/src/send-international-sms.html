To send an international SMS from a business number, check what your contact's country does with texts from abroad: some show your number, some swap in another sender, and some refuse the text. The surest route is a number in your contact's own country, so they see who you are and their replies reach you. Say a London recruitment agency texts candidates from its UK mobile number. A candidate in Berlin gets the text from that number and replies, while a candidate in Paris sees a sender she doesn't know, and her reply never reaches the agency.

Key takeaways
  • The carriers in your contact's country decide what they see and whether they can reply.
  • The UK and the US don't take business texts from foreign numbers. Text UK contacts from a UK mobile and US contacts from a registered US number.
  • Each text is billed at the rate of the country it reaches, and a long text goes out as several.

How much does an international text cost?

On CallForges, each SMS you send or receive is billed per message, at the rate for the country it reaches, and paid from your credits. The country of the number you send from doesn't change the rate.

Length catches people out. One SMS holds 160 characters, or 70 if it has an emoji or letters outside the standard texting alphabet, such as Hindi or Arabic. A longer message goes out in parts of 153 characters (67 with an emoji), and each part counts as one SMS. Say you text a customer in Germany a 200-character appointment reminder: that's two texts at Germany's rate. Add a smiley and the same message runs to three parts at 67 characters each.

A text the checks refuse before it leaves, such as one to a US contact from a UK number, never goes out and costs nothing. A text the carriers fail to deliver after it leaves can carry a small fee for the failed message.

The rules for texting customers in other countries

Check the law where your contact is, because each country writes its own. These are laws, and they bind the business that sends the text. Three you're likely to meet:

  • The UK. Under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, the Information Commissioner's Office guidance on electronic mail marketing counts texts as electronic mail. You need an individual's specific consent before sending them marketing texts, apart from a "soft opt-in" for your own existing customers. Companies can be texted, but sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals.
  • Canada. Canada's anti-spam law defines an electronic message to include text messages. A commercial one needs the person's consent, must identify you and must include a way to unsubscribe (CASL, section 6). It applies when a computer system in Canada is used to send or access the message (section 12), not only when the sender is in Canada.
  • The US. The FCC's rules on telephone solicitations, the 8am to 9pm window at the person's location and the do-not-call rules, cover telemarketing texts to mobile numbers as well as calls (47 CFR 64.1200(c) to (e)). Is missed call text back legal? goes through the US rules in more detail.

Each of these is about marketing, so check how yours treats service texts such as an order update. Some countries also limit marketing texts to certain hours or days, Italy, Brazil and South Africa among them, so check the rules where your contacts are and schedule texts for their daytime.

International SMS FAQ

Can I send an SMS to another country?

Usually, yes, but the carriers there decide whether it shows your number or another sender, or isn't accepted. The US and the UK don't take business texts from foreign numbers, so text those contacts from a number in their country.

What is the international SMS format?

A plus sign, the country code, then the number without its leading 0. A UK mobile, 07700 900123, becomes +44 7700 900123; Australia is +61, and the US and Canada share +1.
