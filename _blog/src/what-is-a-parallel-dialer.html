A parallel dialer is an auto dialer that rings several leads at the same time and connects you to the first person who picks up. The other lines are hung up, so on lists where most calls go unanswered you spend less of each hour waiting for someone to answer.

How does a parallel dialer work?

Most of a manual calling session is waiting. You dial, it rings, nobody picks up, and you dial again. A power dialer removes the dialing by hand, but it still rings one lead at a time. A parallel dialer rings a small batch of leads together and gives you the first one who answers.

In CallForges, each round of a parallel campaign runs like this:

  1. You click Start Campaign. Your own line connects first, so the dialer only rings leads while you are ready to talk.
  2. The dialer rings the next leads on the list. The session bar shows how many lines are live, for example 3/3 lines, and the Active batch table shows each line and its status.
  3. When a line ends without an answer, the dialer rings the next lead in its place, so the batch stays full.
  4. The first person who answers is connected to you. The other lines are hung up at once.
  5. The session bar reads Connected and the lead's contact page opens, so you can see who you are talking to.

When the call ends, the post-call form opens so you can log the outcome and notes. A countdown shows Next call in N secs. Click it to skip the wait, or click Extend for more time. The next round starts when the countdown ends and the form is closed. When no leads are left, the campaign is completed.

You set how many leads ring at once in Number of parallel calls, up to 5 lines. One user runs a parallel campaign at a time, in the browser, with the CallForges tab open. The full walkthrough is in the help article How the parallel dialer works.

Why do sales teams use a parallel dialer?

Cold lists ring out a lot. Many calls end with no answer, a busy line or voicemail, and on a one-line dialer each of those is dead time for the rep. A parallel dialer runs those misses side by side. While one line rings out, the others are still ringing, and a line that fails is refilled with the next lead straight away, so more of a rep's hour goes to conversations.

The trade is preparation time. The lead who answers could be any of the ones ringing, so CallForges opens their contact page the moment they are connected.

What happens with voicemail?

With Auto machine detection on in Settings → Dialer settings, a machine that answers hears the campaign's Voicemail drop, a recorded message you pick when you set up the campaign, and the dialer moves on without you. With detection off, an answering machine counts as an answer and is connected to you the same way a person would be.

What happens when two leads answer at once?

This is the cost of parallel dialing. You can only talk to one lead, so if two pick up within a moment of each other, the first is connected to you and the second has nobody to talk to.

In CallForges, the dialer hangs up a lead and marks it Cancelled when:

  • another lead answered first while this one was still ringing,
  • the lead answered but nobody was free to take the call, or
  • you chose Pause now while the line was ringing.

The second case is the one the person you called notices. They picked up, and the call ended. CallForges does not play them a message before it hangs up.

The Re-dial cancelled leads setting decides what happens next. With Never, the default, a cancelled lead is not called again in that campaign. Set a number instead and the lead goes back to the bottom of the list, up to that many times.

Collisions grow with the number of lines and with how well your list answers. On the Dashboard, the Outbound tab's From dial to outcome card counts them as Picked up, no user free (parallel), so check it after each session.

Is parallel dialing legal?

The US and UK rules below are about what happens to the person who answers. A call that is answered and then dropped is what they target, and who you call matters as much as how you call them.

United States: the Telemarketing Sales Rule

Under the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule, a call is abandoned when a person answers and is not connected to a sales representative within two seconds of finishing their greeting. Abandoning calls is prohibited. The rule's safe harbor protects a seller only if it meets every part of it:

  • no more than 3 percent of calls answered by a person are abandoned, per campaign, measured over each 30-day period;
  • an unanswered call rings for at least 15 seconds or four rings before it is hung up;
  • when no representative is free within two seconds, a recorded message gives the seller's name and phone number;
  • records show all of this.

The FTC's guide, Complying with the Telemarketing Sales Rule, applies this to dialers that call several consumers for each available representative. The rule is aimed at calls to consumers. Most calls between a telemarketer and a business are exempt, with exceptions: calls selling nondurable office or cleaning supplies are covered, and the FTC treats a call to a business line that pitches an employee something for personal use as covered too. The FCC has its own rule under the TCPA, 47 CFR 64.1200. It sets a similar 3 percent limit and two-second test for telemarketing calls, requires an identification and opt-out message, and bars hanging up an unanswered telemarketing call before 15 seconds or four rings. Its text has no business-to-business exemption, so check it with your counsel even for B2B lists.

United Kingdom: Ofcom's rules on silent and abandoned calls

Ofcom's statement of policy on persistent misuse, which applies from 1 March 2017, treats both silent and abandoned calls as misuse. A silent call includes one where the caller hangs up as soon as, or shortly after, the person picks up, with no message. An abandoned call is one where a recorded message plays because no agent is free. Ofcom says callers should make neither, and it sees silent calls as the more harmful kind.

Where there are not enough agents, Ofcom expects an information message within two seconds that says who called and gives a number to ring to decline further calls. Ofcom may act on any caller who makes abandoned calls, and the abandoned call rate is one factor in which cases it pursues. It also weighs calls cut off before they have rung for 15 seconds, repeat calls to the same number within 72 hours without a live agent guaranteed, and answering machine detection that mistakes a person for a machine.

The statement is written mainly about calls to consumers, but Ofcom's powers cover harm to "someone else, especially consumers", and it sets out no exemption for business numbers.

What this means for a CallForges parallel campaign

Read against these rules, a second answer that CallForges hangs up with no message is an abandoned call under the TSR's definition and a silent call under Ofcom's. A line that is still ringing when another lead answers is hung up at once, which can be before it has rung for 15 seconds. That points the parallel dialer at business-to-business calling, where the TSR mostly does not apply. For consumer lists in the US or the UK, use the power dialer, which keeps you on the line for every call it places. Our power dialer vs parallel dialer guide compares the two.

Settings that help, whoever you call:

  • Keep Number of parallel calls low. Fewer lines means fewer collisions.
  • Leave Re-dial cancelled leads at Never unless you have a reason to call cut leads again soon.
  • Turn on Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists and set calling hours with Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours when you create the campaign.

Consent, calling hours and do-not-call rules are your responsibility under the CallForges Terms.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules depend on who you call, what you sell and where. Check with a lawyer who knows telemarketing law before you run any auto dialer on consumer lists.

Who should use a parallel dialer?

A parallel dialer suits teams with a lot of numbers to get through and a low chance that any one call is answered:

  • SDR teams working large business-to-business lists, such as company main lines and older data.
  • Agencies running outbound for clients.
  • Founders and small teams who block an hour for prospecting.
  • Anyone working back through leads who never answered, with a follow-up campaign built from them.

It is a poor fit for consumer lists in the US or the UK, for the reasons above, and for warm leads such as inbound leads and booked callbacks, which tend to answer and so collide more. For those, and for named accounts you want to research before each call, the power dialer is the better tool.

How do you set up a parallel dialer in CallForges?

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Parallel Dialer. Then set Number of parallel calls, Re-dial cancelled leads and, if you like, a Voicemail drop.
  3. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see, and set the Cooldown duration between two calls.
  4. Under Audience, pick a contact list or upload a CSV file with a phone number column.
  5. Choose the recording, Do Not Call and calling hours options, then click Create Campaign.
  6. Open the campaign, allow the browser to use your microphone, and click Start Campaign.

The dialer type can't be changed after the campaign is created, so pick it per list. Create a campaign covers every option on the form.
