You're on a roof with both hands busy when your phone buzzes in your pocket. You get down the ladder ten minutes later, and the caller has moved on to the next roofer on Google. Missed call text back is a phone system feature that texts that caller for you. If nobody answers, it sends a short text from the number they called, a few seconds later, so they hear from you before they dial someone else.

Key takeaways
  • Missed call text back texts a caller you didn't answer, from the number they rang, and the conversation moves to text.
  • It pays off when someone reads the replies within the hour. A text promising a callback that never comes leaves a worse impression than voicemail.
  • A US business number needs carrier registration (A2P 10DLC) before it can text, and the review usually takes 1 to 3 weeks.

How does missed call text back work?

It sits in your call routing, the rules that decide who rings and what happens when nobody answers:

  1. The call rings your phone, or your team's phones, for a set time.
  2. Nobody picks up, so the call takes the unanswered route, often to voicemail.
  3. The system texts the caller from the number they dialed, at once or after a delay you set, without holding up the call.
  4. The caller replies, and whoever on your team is free answers.

Almost everyone can receive a text: 98% of US adults own a cellphone of some kind (Pew Research Center, 2026). They can reply when it suits them, and you both skip phone tag.

Keep voicemail on anyway, for the calls a text can't reach:

  • The caller hid their number.
  • They rang from a landline or an office switchboard.
  • They replied STOP to an earlier text from you and haven't opted back in.

When it helps, and when it annoys

It helps most when one person runs the phone and the job at once, like a roofer on a ladder or a stylist with a client in the chair. The caller wanted a quote or a booking and would have rung the next name on the list. A text saying you saw the call, and asking what they need, keeps them with you.

It annoys when it promises something nobody does. Say the text promises a callback in 10 minutes at 4pm, and you ring the next morning: you've broken your first promise to that customer. Repeat callers are the other trap: three calls in a row can bring three identical texts, which reads like a bot.

Some callers need a person now. A tenant with water coming through the ceiling doesn't want "Reply with your address". Send urgent lines to a phone someone carries, and keep the text for calls that can wait an hour. If you'd rather after-hours callers talked to something, an AI receptionist answers the call itself.

We'd turn it on for any number new customers call when nobody can always pick up, and send the text within the first minute, while the caller is still deciding who to ring next. Add a few minutes' delay only if your team often calls back straight away, so nobody gets your text and your call at once. The trade-off is work: every text opens a conversation someone has to finish. If replies would sit unread until Friday, fix that first.

What should a missed call text say?

Type "missed call text message" into Google and "scam" is among the first suggestions, so expect some callers to doubt your text. Make it read like a person at the business they meant to call:

  • Your business name in the first few words.
  • The real reason you missed the call, in a few words.
  • One next step, usually "reply here with what you need".
  • A promise you'll keep. Write "today" only if you mean today.

Keep it under 160 characters, the size of one text. An emoji cuts that to 70, so one smiley can split your message into two or three texts, each charged. In the US, add "Reply STOP to opt out": the sample messages you register with the carriers need it, and your real texts should match them.

Three we'd start from, with your business name in the brackets:

  • "Hi, it's Dave at [Business]. I'm up on a roof and missed your call. Text me your address and the job, and I'll call when I'm down. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • "Hi, this is Maria at [Business]. I'm with a client. Reply with the day you'd like to come in and I'll text you my free times. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • "Thanks for calling [Business]. We're closed until 8am. Reply with your name and what you need, and we'll call you first thing. Reply STOP to opt out."

Do you need to register to text back in the US?

Yes, for a US local number. US carriers block business texts from a local number that isn't registered. The registration is called A2P 10DLC: A2P means application to person, a business system texting someone, and 10DLC is an ordinary 10-digit local number.

Registration is carrier policy. The mobile networks set the rule and can change it, and they want to know which business stands behind a number before its texts reach their customers. You register your business, then a campaign: what you'll send, to whom, and how people agreed to get it. The review usually takes 1 to 3 weeks. A rejection means fixing what they flagged and paying the review fee again. Start before you build anything, since no text goes out until you're approved.

Is missed call text back legal?

If you text people in the US, the law that applies is the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA). The FCC's rules under it say a call to a cell phone made with an autodialer or a prerecorded voice needs the person's prior express consent, and their prior express written consent if it advertises or sells. The same rules cover texts, and they let people take back consent by replying words such as "stop" or "unsubscribe" (47 CFR 64.1200(a), eCFR, 2026).

For a reply to someone who rang you a minute ago, the answer varies by situation. It turns on how your system sends the text and what it says, and state laws can add rules of their own. We'd keep the text about their call, with no offers in it, and stop the moment someone replies STOP.

This is general information, not legal advice.
