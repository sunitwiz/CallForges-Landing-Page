A power dialer rings one lead at a time, while a parallel dialer rings several leads at once and connects you to the first who answers. Use the parallel dialer for large business lists where most calls go unanswered, and the power dialer when your leads tend to answer or when you call consumers.

What is the difference between a power dialer and a parallel dialer?

Both are auto dialers. They work through a list for you, so nobody types numbers. The difference is how many leads ring at once, and that one choice changes what a lead hears when they pick up.

How does a power dialer work?

A power dialer calls the leads on your list one after another. In CallForges, you click Start Campaign and the dialer calls the lead at the top of the dial order. Your own line places each call, so when the lead answers you are already there. The strip shows Connected and the lead's contact page opens.

When the call ends, you go back to the campaign and the strip shows Wrapping up… with a countdown to the next call. Its length comes from the campaign's wrap-up and cooldown settings. Click the countdown to skip it, or click Extend for more time. Log outcome opens the post-call form, and the countdown waits while the form is open. With Auto-advance on, the next lead rings when the countdown ends. With it off, you click Dial next for each call.

If you reach a voicemail box, pick one of your recorded messages and click Drop voicemail. The message plays into the box, your side of the call ends, and the campaign carries on to the next lead.

Leads are dialed top to bottom, and you can drag rows in Edit campaign to change the order before you start.

How does a parallel dialer work?

A parallel dialer rings a batch of leads at once. In CallForges, your own line connects first. The dialer then rings the next leads on the list, up to the number you set, and refills any line that ends without an answer. The first person who answers is connected to you, every other line is hung up, and the lead's contact page opens.

After the call, the post-call form opens and a countdown runs before the next round. With Auto machine detection on in Settings → Dialer settings, an answering machine hears the campaign's Voicemail drop and the dialer moves on without you. One user runs a parallel campaign at a time.

One part needs attention before you choose it. When two leads answer at nearly the same moment, the second one is hung up with no message and marked Cancelled. The Re-dial cancelled leads setting decides whether that lead is called again, and it defaults to Never. What is a parallel dialer? walks through a full round and every case where a line is cut.

When is a power dialer the better choice?

  • You call consumers. In the US and the UK, the rules on abandoned and silent calls are written mostly with consumers in mind. A power dialer keeps you on the line for every call, so the dialing itself does not create those calls.
  • Your leads tend to answer. Warm inbound leads, booked callbacks, existing customers and event follow-ups pick up often. On a parallel dialer that means more collisions and more cut calls.
  • You want to choose who comes next. The power dialer works top to bottom in the order you set, so you can put your best accounts first and prepare for each one.

The power dialer is also the simpler place to start for a rep who is new to auto dialers. Pausing has no options to weigh: the current call finishes and nothing new is dialed.

When is a parallel dialer the better choice?

The parallel dialer earns its place on large business-to-business lists where most calls ring out, reach a busy line or go to voicemail. On those lists a one-line dialer spends much of the hour on misses. Ringing several leads at once runs the misses side by side, so more of the hour goes to conversations.

Typical users are SDR teams working cold B2B data, agencies prospecting for clients and founders who block an hour for outbound. Start with a low Number of parallel calls, then check the Outbound tab of the Dashboard. Its From dial to outcome card counts answered calls that had nobody free as Picked up, no user free (parallel). If that count climbs, use fewer lines or move the list to the power dialer.

Which dialer is easier to keep compliant?

The power dialer. You are on the line for every call it places, so an answered call reaches you. The parallel dialer can produce the calls regulators look for.

Under the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule, a call is abandoned when the person who answers is not connected to a sales representative within two seconds of their greeting. The rule's safe harbor allows no more than 3 percent of answered calls to be abandoned per campaign over each 30-day period, and it expects a recorded message with the seller's name and number when nobody is free. In the UK, Ofcom's policy on persistent misuse counts a call that is hung up just after the person picks up, with no message, as a silent call, and treats silent calls as the more harmful kind.

A second answer in a CallForges parallel campaign is hung up with no message, so count it as one of those calls. Most business-to-business calls are exempt from the TSR, which is why the parallel dialer fits B2B lists. The FCC's abandonment rule in 47 CFR 64.1200 and Ofcom's policy set out no business exemption, so check your lists with counsel. Can you use both dialers in CallForges?

Yes. Every paid plan includes the power dialer with voicemail drop and the parallel dialer, and campaigns start once you choose a plan. See pricing for what each plan adds, and compare for how CallForges stacks up against other dialers.

You choose the dialer per campaign under Dialer Type when you create it, and it can't be changed afterwards. To move leads from one dialer to the other, open a campaign, tick the leads you want (filter by status first), and click Create campaign. The form opens as a follow-up with the same settings and those leads as the audience, and you can change anything before you create it, the dialer type included.

One way to use both: work a cold business list on the parallel dialer. Then tick the leads who asked you to call back, build a follow-up from them and call them on the power dialer, one at a time. The steps are in Call the same leads again and Start, pause, resume or stop a campaign.
