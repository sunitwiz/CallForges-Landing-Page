It's 2 January, your first morning back, and the office line holds 40 voicemails from callers who heard "we're open, leave a message" all week. A holiday voicemail greeting for business tells callers you're closed, the day and date you're back, and what to do if it can't wait, in under 30 seconds. Copy one of the 10 examples here, swap in your dates, and let your phone system switch it on and off by date.

Key takeaways
  • A holiday greeting needs the day and date you're back, what to do if it's urgent, and whether anyone will hear a message before then.
  • Federal and bank holiday lists are a calendar. A private business picks its own closures, so the greeting is where callers learn yours.
  • Switch the greeting on and off by date instead of by hand, so it can't stay on until mid-January.

What to say in a business holiday voicemail message

A business holiday voicemail message answers three questions, and we'd put them in this order:

  • The day and date you're back: "Monday, January 4, at 8am". "After the holidays" means a different day to each caller, and the weekday gives away a recording left over from another year.
  • A route for urgent calls: an on-call number, an inbox someone checks, or a plain "we can't help until the 4th", which at least tells the caller to try someone else.
  • Your plan for messages: leave one or not, and when you'll return it. If nobody listens until you're back, say so.

Cut the rest. A minute of holiday wishes, or music before the beep, makes callers wait for the one fact they rang for. Skip your year-round hours and any sales pitch. Jokes wear thin on a customer who calls three times in a week. Your own voice sounds warmer than a typed message the phone system reads out, though the typed one is quicker to fix when a date changes.

10 holiday voicemail greeting examples for business

Swap in your business name and dates. Each reads aloud in under 30 seconds.

Generic holiday voicemail greeting (any holiday)

It names no holiday, so it fits every closure and every caller. Change the date and keep the weekday.

"Thanks for calling [Business]. We're closed today for the holiday and back on [weekday, date] at [time]. Leave your name, number and a short message, and we'll call you back that day. If it can't wait, [your urgent option]."

Thanksgiving voicemail greeting

"Thanks for calling [Business]. We're closed for Thanksgiving on Thursday, November 26, and Friday, November 27, and back Monday, November 30, at 8am. Leave your name, number and what you need, and we'll call you back Monday morning. Happy Thanksgiving."

Christmas to New Year closure greeting

"You've reached [Business]. We're closed from Thursday, December 24, through Friday, January 1, and back Monday, January 4, at 8am. Leave a message and we'll return calls that week. Happy holidays."

Christmas bank holiday message (UK)

"Thank you for calling [Business]. We're closed for the Christmas bank holidays on Friday 25 and Monday 28 December, and open again on Tuesday 29 December at 9am. Please leave a message and we'll ring you back then. Merry Christmas."

Closed for the holiday: one-day message

"Thanks for calling [Business]. We're closed today, Monday, January 18, for Martin Luther King Jr. Day. We're back tomorrow, Tuesday, January 19, at 9am. Leave a message and we'll call you then."

Holiday hours message: closing early

"Thanks for calling [Business]. We closed early today, Thursday, December 31, for New Year's Eve, and we're closed tomorrow too. We're back Monday, January 4, at 9am. Leave a message and we'll call you that morning."

Holiday voicemail with an emergency number

"You've reached [Business]. Our office is closed until Monday, January 4. If you have no heat or water is leaking, call our on-call technician at [number], day or night. If anyone is in danger, hang up and call 911. For anything else, leave a message and we'll call you back on the 4th."

Out of office voicemail for the holidays

"We're back on Monday, January 4. Until then, [Business] is closed for the holidays. Leave your name and number, and we'll call you that morning."

Vacation voicemail greeting for a small team

"Hi, it's Sam at [Business]. I'm off until Monday, January 4. Leave your name and number and I'll call you back that week. Happy holidays."

Holiday schedule message before you close

This one plays before your team's phones ring, while you're still open.

"Thanks for calling [Business]. Before we put you through: we're closed from December 24 to January 1, so if you need anything before the new year, let us know today."

Christmas and New Year voicemail greetings

The Christmas break spans two holidays and a weekend, so callers can't guess which days you're in. In 2026, Christmas Day and New Year's Day both fall on a Friday and the UK's Boxing Day bank holiday moves to Monday 28 December, so last year's recording names the wrong days. The closure greeting above covers a business shut for the whole break. If you close early on Christmas Eve or open in the week between, Christmas voicemail greeting examples for business has a script for each day, plus Boxing Day for UK offices, an on-call version and a Christmas out of office phone message.
