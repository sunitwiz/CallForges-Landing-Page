A power dialer is an auto dialer that calls the leads on your list one at a time and starts the next call as soon as the last one ends. Nobody types numbers, and you are on the line before every lead answers.

How does a power dialer work?

Picture a rep with a list of 80 leads and a phone. Without a dialer, each call means finding the next row, copying the number, dialing it, waiting, and then writing notes before starting again. A lot of the hour goes to the steps around the calls.

A power dialer takes over those steps. You load a list, start a session, and the dialer works down the list for you:

  1. It calls the first lead. Your own line places the call, so you hear it ring.
  2. If the lead answers, you are already there. Their contact record opens so you can see who you are talking to.
  3. When the call ends, you log the outcome and a short countdown starts.
  4. When the countdown ends, the dialer calls the next lead.

That loop repeats until the list is done or you stop. Because it dials one lead at a time, a power dialer never rings someone you are not ready to talk to. This is the main difference from dialers that ring several leads at once, which we cover below.

In CallForges, the power dialer runs as a campaign. You click Start Campaign, the strip shows Power Dialing…, and it changes to Connected when the lead picks up. After the call the strip shows Wrapping up… with a countdown to the next call.

What happens between calls?

The time between calls is where a power dialer saves you the most, and also where reps need some control. A good power dialer gives you a pause long enough to write notes, and an easy way to skip it when you are ready.

Here is what you can do during the countdown in CallForges:

  • Skip it. Click the countdown (Skip Cooldown) to dial the next lead now.
  • Add time. Click +10s when you need a few more seconds.
  • Log the call. Click Log outcome to open the post-call form. The countdown waits while the form is open, and the strip reads Next call after you log the outcome.

The length of the pause comes from the campaign's wrap-up and cooldown settings, so a manager can set a pace that fits the team. If a rep prefers to start every call by hand, turn Auto-advance off and they click Dial next each time.

The post-call form is where the call becomes data. Reps pick a Disposition, add a Note and a Rating if they like, and can tick Add to Do Not Call list for a lead who asks not to be called again. A manager can make the disposition required, so no call goes unlogged.

How does voicemail drop work on a power dialer?

Many calls on any cold list end at a voicemail box. Leaving the same message by hand, over and over, takes time and energy. Voicemail drop fixes that.

You record your messages once, in Settings → My voicemail → Voicemail drops. On a call, when you hear the voicemail greeting, pick one of your messages and click Drop voicemail. The message plays into the lead's voicemail box, your side of the call ends, and the campaign carries on to the next lead. You never wait for the beep.

Keep two or three messages ready, for example one for a first touch and one for a follow-up, so the message fits the lead. Our guide to the power dialer vs parallel dialer shows how voicemail works on each.

How is a power dialer different from other dialers?

There are a few kinds of dialer, and the names get mixed up. This table shows the common ones side by side:

Is the rep on the line when a lead answers?YesYesThe first lead to answer is connectedOnly if an agent is free

Best forA few high-value callsWarm leads, follow-ups, consumer lists, named accountsLarge business lists where most calls go unansweredLarge call centers with many agents

A parallel dialer rings several leads together and connects you to the first one who answers. It suits big cold B2B lists. A predictive dialer guesses how many lines to ring based on how many agents will be free, which works best with a large team. Our post on power dialer vs predictive dialer goes deeper on that one.

The power dialer sits in the middle. It cuts out the manual work, and the rep still talks to every lead who picks up.

Who should use a power dialer?

A power dialer fits most teams that make outbound calls every day. It is a strong choice when:

  • Your leads tend to answer. Inbound leads, booked callbacks, trial users and event follow-ups pick up often. One line at a time means every answer reaches you.
  • You call consumers. In the US and the UK, the rules on abandoned and silent calls are written mostly with consumers in mind. A power dialer places one call at a time with you on the line, so the dialing pattern does not create those calls.
  • Every lead matters. For named accounts, you can order the list so your best accounts come first, and glance at the contact record before each call.
  • Your reps are new to dialers. There is nothing to tune beyond the pause between calls, so a new rep can start on day one.

Sales teams, agency callers, recruiters, real estate agents and founders doing their own outbound all use power dialers for these reasons. If your list is large and cold and most calls go unanswered, look at the parallel dialer for that list and keep the power dialer for the leads who respond.

Is a power dialer legal?

Using a dialer is legal in the US and the UK. What the law looks at is who you call, whether you have the right to call them, when you call, and what happens when they answer.

Under the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule, a call is abandoned when the person who answers is not connected to a sales representative within two seconds of their greeting. The FCC has a similar rule in 47 CFR 64.1200. Both target dialers that ring more people than there are reps to talk to. A power dialer places one call at a time from the rep's own line, so the rep is there when the lead answers.

The other rules still apply to every call, however it is dialed: consent where it is required, do-not-call registers, calling hours, and the rules on recorded messages. In the US, the FTC's guide to the Telemarketing Sales Rule is a good place to start. In the UK, the ICO's guidance on live marketing calls covers the Telephone Preference Service and consent.

CallForges campaigns have settings that help: Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists skips anyone on your Do Not Call list, and Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours only dials leads inside the days and hours you set, in their own time zone.

This is general information, not legal advice. The rules depend on who you call, what you sell and where. Consent, calling hours and do-not-call rules are your responsibility under the CallForges Terms.

How do you set up a power dialer in CallForges?

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign. Click the name at the top to rename it.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Power Dialer.
  3. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads will see, and set the Cooldown duration between two calls.
  4. Under Audience, click Select a list to use a contact list, or Upload leads to import a CSV file with a phone number column.
  5. Turn on Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists, set your calling hours, and choose whether to record calls. Then click Create Campaign.
  6. To change the call order, click Edit and drag the rows. Leads are dialed top to bottom.
  7. Allow the browser to use your microphone and click Start Campaign.

Click Pause for a break: the current call finishes and nothing new is dialed. Resume picks up at the next lead. When you want to call back everyone who didn't answer, tick those leads, click Create campaign, and a follow-up campaign opens with the same settings. The full steps are in Create a campaign and Start, pause, resume or stop a campaign.
