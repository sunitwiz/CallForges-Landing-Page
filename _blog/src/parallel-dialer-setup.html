To set up a parallel dialer in CallForges, create a campaign, choose Parallel Dialer, set the Number of parallel calls, pick the number you call from and add your leads. Start with few lines, and take a break with Pause now or Pause after this round. Pause never cuts a call you are on.

You need a paid plan, a role that can create and run campaigns, an active phone number, a list of leads and a browser that may use your microphone.

  • A paid plan. Campaigns, the parallel dialer included, start with your paid plan. During the free trial the Campaigns page reads Campaigns start with your plan. Every paid plan includes the parallel dialer: see pricing.
  • The right role. Setting up needs Create and edit campaigns, and starting needs Run campaigns. Owners, admins and members have both by default.
  • An active phone number. Without one, the form shows You need an active phone number to place calls. with Get a number.
  • Your leads. A contact list in CallForges, or a CSV file with at least a phone number column.

How do you set up a parallel dialer campaign in CallForges?

You set it up on one form: create a campaign, choose Parallel Dialer, fill in the three parallel settings, then pick your number, your leads and your calling options.

  1. Open Campaigns and click Create campaign. The form opens as Untitled Campaign. Click the name at the top to rename it.
  2. Under Dialer Type, choose Parallel Dialer.
  3. In Number of parallel calls, choose how many leads ring at once.
  4. In Re-dial cancelled leads, choose how many times a lead the dialer cut is called again. Never is the default.
  5. Optionally, pick a Voicemail drop. It plays when an answering machine picks up, then the dialer moves on.
  6. Under Call Via, pick the number your leads see. Your own numbers are offered first.
  7. Set Cooldown duration between two calls, in seconds.
  8. Under Audience, add your leads.
  9. Choose the recording, Do Not Call and calling hours options at the bottom, then click Create Campaign.

If something is missing, CallForges tells you what to add, such as a contact list or a valid calling window. The campaign opens as a Draft, and nothing is dialed until you start it.

The dialer type can't be changed later. The caller number, the cooldown and the calling window can: open the campaign and click Settings. The help articles How the parallel dialer works and Create a campaign walk through each field, with screenshots.

How many lines should ring at once?

Start low, and add lines only while hardly any leads are being cut. You can talk to one lead at a time, so each extra line is one more lead who may answer while you are already talking.

The first person who picks up is connected to you, and every other line is hung up. The dialer hangs up a ringing lead and marks it Cancelled when:

  • another lead answered first,
  • the lead answered but nobody was free to take the call, or
  • you chose Pause now while the line was ringing.

In the second case, the lead picked up and the call ended. The parallel dialer is built for large lists where most calls go unanswered. For leads who tend to pick up, such as warm leads, use fewer lines or the power dialer. Power dialer vs parallel dialer compares the two. After a session, open the campaign's leads. If many show Cancelled, use fewer lines on your next campaign.

Should you re-dial cancelled leads?

With Never, the default, a cut lead is not called again. With a number, it goes back to the bottom of the list, up to that many times, and its status shows how many re-dials are left.

Keep Never if you'd rather not ring someone again soon after a call ended as they picked up. Set a number when your list is short and you want every lead reached in this campaign. You can also call cut leads later: tick them in the campaign's list (filter by status first) and click Create campaign. The form opens with the same settings and those leads; change what you need and click Create Campaign. See Call the same leads again.

Which list should you load?

Load a large list where most calls go unanswered, such as cold business-to-business data, and put the leads you most want to reach at the top. That is where ringing several lines at once saves the most time.

  • Select a list uses a contact list you already have. Open List and pick one; each shows how many contacts it holds.
  • Upload leads imports a CSV file. CallForges detects the columns, and a file saved from Excel works as it is. Rows without a phone number are skipped, and existing contacts are matched by phone, then by email. Choose where the contacts go under Add to, click Import, then Done.

Leads are dialed top to bottom. To change the order, open the campaign, click Edit and drag rows. A contact already in the campaign is not added twice.

The options at the bottom of the form

  • Respect Do-Not-Call (DNC) lists: contacts on your Do Not Call list are not dialed, and show Do not call.
  • Use the lead/contact's local time zone to call during their active hours: pick the Active Days, a Start Time and an End Time, in the lead's own local hours. A lead whose turn comes outside them shows Out of timezone and is not called again in this campaign. A US number uses its area code's time zone.
  • Enable default call recording for all calls in this campaign: records every connected call.

Start the campaign during your leads' hours, or many of them may be skipped as Out of timezone.

Who you call matters for the rules

A lead hung up just after answering is what the rules on abandoned and silent calls target. In the US, the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule limits abandoned telemarketing calls, but most business-to-business calls are exempt. The FCC's rule, 47 CFR 64.1200, limits them too, and its text has no business-to-business exemption. In the UK, Ofcom's policy on persistent misuse counts a call hung up as the person picks up as a silent call, and is written mainly about calls to consumers. That is one reason to keep the parallel dialer for business lists and use the power dialer for consumer lists. Neither choice is a safe harbor, so check your lists with counsel. How do you set up the voicemail drop?

Pick a recording under Voicemail drop when you create the campaign, and turn on Auto machine detection in Settings → Dialer settings. With both on, an answering machine that picks up hears your message, the lead shows Voicemail dropped, and the dialer moves on without you.

With Auto machine detection off, an answering machine counts as an answer. It is connected to you the same way a person would be, and the other lines are hung up, just as when a person answers.

What happens when you click Start Campaign?

Your own line connects first, then the dialer rings a batch of leads and connects you to the first one who answers. So the dialer only rings leads while you are ready to talk.

  1. The session bar shows how many lines are live, for example 3/3 lines, and the Active batch table shows each line and its status.
  2. When a line ends without an answer, the dialer rings the next lead in its place, so the batch stays full.
  3. When someone answers, the bar reads Connected with a timer, and CallForges opens the lead's contact page, so you know who you are talking to.
  4. After the call, the post-call form opens for the outcome and notes (or click Log outcome). The countdown shows Next call in N secs: click it to Skip Cooldown, or click Extend for more time.
  5. The next round starts when the countdown ends and the form is closed. When no leads are left, the campaign is completed.

Pause now or Pause after this round: which should you pick?

Pick Pause after this round when you can take one more conversation, and Pause now when you have to stop straight away. A call you are already on is never cut by Pause.

CallForges also pauses for you when you close or reload the tab (ringing lines are hung up), when the workspace runs out of credits (add credits, then resume), and when the campaign's phone number is deleted (attach a new one in Settings first).
