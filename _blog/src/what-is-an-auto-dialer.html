An auto dialer is software that calls the numbers on your list for you and puts a rep through when someone answers. It dials each number, checks whether a person or a voicemail picked up, and moves to the next lead when the call ends. Picture four reps with a week to call 600 trade-show leads from one spreadsheet: dialed by hand, a good part of each day goes to finding the next number and typing it in.

Key takeaways
  • An auto dialer calls your list in a loop and puts the rep through each time a person answers.
  • For most small teams we'd start with a power dialer, and add a parallel dialer for big cold business lists.
  • Using a dialer is legal. The rules are about who you call, consent, and what happens when someone answers.

How does an auto dialer work?

An auto dialer runs one loop until your list is done: it calls a number, puts the rep through when a person picks up, and dials the next lead once the call ends. Step by step:

  1. You load a list of leads and their phone numbers into a campaign, usually from your CRM or a spreadsheet, pick a few settings and start a session.
  2. The dialer calls the next number on the list from your business number, so the rep never types a digit.
  3. The line rings for a set time. If nobody picks up or the line is busy, the dialer logs the result and goes on to the next lead.
  4. When the call is answered, the dialer works out whether it's a person or a voicemail greeting. Some dialers detect an answering machine and leave a recorded message on their own; on others the rep hears the greeting and drops the recording with one click.
  5. A person answered, so the dialer connects them to the rep with the lead's record on screen.
  6. The call ends, the rep logs the outcome, and after a short pause the dialer calls the next lead.

The types of dialer below differ at step 2, in how many numbers ring at once. The dialer also logs every call, so a manager can see who was called and what happened.

As an example, say a rep spends 30 seconds finding and typing each number. At 150 dials a day, that's more than an hour of typing, and an auto dialer gives that hour back to conversations.

Types of auto dialer

The four types you'll see most often differ in how many calls ring at once for each rep, and who decides:

TypeHow it dialsRep on the line when a lead answers?Best for
Preview dialerShows the rep the next lead, and the rep clicks to callYesSmall lists of high-value accounts
Power dialerOne call at a time, the next one starts when the last endsYesMost sales teams, warm leads, consumer lists
Parallel dialerSeveral leads at once for one rep, the first answer is connectedYes, for the first answerLarge business lists where most calls go unanswered
Predictive dialerSeveral calls per agent, timed by an algorithmOnly if an agent is freeLarge call centers with many agents

You may also hear "progressive dialer". It usually means the same thing as a power dialer: one call per rep, placed when the rep is free.

For most small teams, we'd start with a power dialer. It removes the dialing and keeps the rep on every call it places. Add a parallel dialer when one rep has a big cold list where most calls ring out. It gets through that list faster, and the cost is that it hangs up on the second person when two answer at once. Who needs an auto dialer?

Any team whose reps spend more of the day typing numbers than talking. That's usually SDR and inside sales teams on prospect lists, agencies running outbound for clients, and real estate and insurance agents calling leads, past clients and renewals.

If a rep makes five calls a day to accounts they've researched for an hour each, skip it: dialing by hand from the CRM is fine. Once a rep is making a few dozen dials a day, we'd want a dialer doing the dialing.

Is an auto dialer legal?

Yes, using one is legal. The rules are about how you use it: who you call and when, whether you need their consent, and what happens when a person answers.

In the US, the TCPA, a federal law, restricts calls made with an "automatic telephone dialing system". In Facebook v. Duguid (2021), the Supreme Court held that this term covers equipment that can store or produce numbers using a random or sequential number generator. Two more federal rules, both law, apply whatever equipment you use. The FCC's rules in 47 CFR 64.1200 (as amended in 2025) cover:

  • prerecorded messages;
  • abandoned calls;
  • calling hours;
  • the do-not-call registry.

The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule adds rules for telemarketing to consumers, and most business-to-business calls are exempt from it.

In the UK, the rules on live marketing calls are law, and the ICO's guidance on live marketing calls explains the Telephone Preference Service and consent. Ofcom's policy on persistent misuse, a regulator's enforcement policy, covers silent and abandoned calls.

The dialer type matters for abandoned calls. A power dialer keeps the rep on the line for every call it places. A dialer that rings several leads at once can hang up on someone who answers, and that's the call the abandoned-call rules target. Is parallel dialing legal? goes through each rule.
