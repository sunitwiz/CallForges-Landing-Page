Dialpad vs Aircall pricing

Dialpad sells four products, so its price depends on which one has the feature you need. Connect, the business phone, costs $15 per user a month billed annually ($27 monthly) on Standard and $25 ($35) on Pro. Sell, for outbound teams, runs $39, $59 and $79 billed annually, and Support, the contact center, runs $89, $119 and $149. Premium plans on Sell and Support are annual only, and the page warns that your price may differ by region and currency. It doesn't list a seat minimum (Dialpad pricing, checked November 2026).

Aircall publishes two plans: Essentials at $30 per license a month and Professional at $50, both billed annually. Its FAQ says every plan needs at least three licenses, whatever the billing cycle, and that annual billing saves 25%, but the page shows no monthly price (Aircall pricing, checked November 2026). Pay a year upfront and your licenses are locked for the year (Aircall help center).

Two reps show the gap. On Dialpad Connect Standard they pay $30 a month, with no dialer. Aircall bills them for three licenses: $90 on Essentials, or $150 on Professional to get the Power Dialer.

Which one has the better dialer?

Between the two, Aircall. Professional, at $50, adds a Power Dialer that imports up to 1,000 numbers and dials them in sequence, with time between calls for notes and tags, on desktop and web. The same plan adds voicemail drop and outbound campaigns with automated retries. On Essentials, outbound campaigns are for Aircall's AI Voice Agents only (Aircall pricing).

Dialpad keeps its dialing tools in pricier products. No Connect plan lists a dialer. Sell Advanced, at $59, includes what Dialpad calls Reach Booster: local presence dialing and voicemail drop. The first plan that names a Power Dialer is Support Essentials, at $89 per user a month billed annually or $109 monthly (Dialpad pricing).

Neither pricing page lists a dialer that rings several leads at once. A power dialer rings one lead, waits out the rings and the voicemail greeting, then rings the next. Say a rep works 80 leads and one in eight picks up: most of the afternoon is spent listening to rings. A parallel dialer rings several leads together and puts the rep through to whoever answers first. We'd weigh the dialer above anything else on a plan page, because it sets how many conversations each rep has in a day.

AI notes, texting and AI agents

Between the two, Dialpad gives you more AI for the money. Connect Standard, at $15, includes real-time transcripts and instant call summaries, which Dialpad calls AI Recaps. Its AI agents, sold as Resolve, are priced by custom quote and charged per conversation in which the AI does some work (Dialpad pricing).

Aircall Essentials includes call recordings and AI transcripts. Call summaries there need the AI Assist add-on, whose price the page doesn't show, and they're included on Professional. Real-time coaching is AI Assist Pro, $49 per seat a month on top. Every Aircall plan includes 50 AI Voice Agent minutes a month, then $0.29 a minute pay as you go, or bundles that work out to about $0.19 (Aircall pricing).

Both text. Aircall includes SMS and MMS from Essentials in 14 countries, with 4,000 outbound texts per user a month, delivered inside one country except between the US and Canada, and quotes the rates on request. Dialpad builds texting into Connect and carries it into Sell and Support, and walks you through A2P 10DLC, the carriers' registration for business texts from US local numbers (Dialpad business texting).
