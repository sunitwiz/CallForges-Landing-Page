Where dealership calls go wrong

Most callers don't want sales. In a study of 1,000 calls to US dealers, 74% of callers asked about parts and service and only 14% about new or used cars. One call in six (16%) went unanswered, and in 63% nobody tried to set an appointment (WardsAuto, 2014). It's an old study, but we'd look for the three failures below first.

Sales leads get lost. An internet shopper calls the number on the listing, the front desk can't find the salesperson who sent the e-mail, and the message goes on a sticky note. By the time anyone calls back, the shopper has driven the same truck at another store.

In service, the morning rush puts every advisor on the drive writing up cars, so "is my car ready?" calls ring out.

Parts calls bounce. A body shop calling for a part gets the switchboard, then sales, then parts, and the mechanic who calls twice a day goes through the same transfers every time.

How to route sales, service and parts

Put a short phone menu, an IVR, on your main number. We'd keep it to three keys in the order callers expect: 1 for sales, 2 for service, 3 for parts. Every extra key costs every caller time, so send finance calls through sales, who know the deal.

Say the department before the key ("For service, press 2"), so the caller hears what they want first. Give regulars a way around it: a direct service number on repair orders and a parts number on invoices, each with its own routing. Some callers never press a key, so send that path to a person at the front desk. How to set up an IVR phone menu covers the menu itself.

Internet leads, missed calls and texts

We'd call web leads within minutes, from a dialer. A power dialer suits today's leads: it rings one lead at a time and dials the next when you hang up. Leads that went quiet weeks ago suit a parallel dialer, which rings several at once and connects you to the first person who answers (power vs parallel dialer).

A text after a missed call keeps the customer talking to you: "Sorry we missed you. Reply here and the parts counter will text you back." Advisors can text "your car is ready" from a shared inbox, and the reply lands in the same conversation.
