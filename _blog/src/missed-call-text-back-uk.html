Missed call text back works in the UK as it does anywhere: when nobody answers, your phone system texts the caller a few seconds later, so they hear from you before they ring someone else. In the UK it has to come from a mobile number: 01 and 02 numbers are landlines and most can't send texts, so the text goes from a 07 number while customers keep ringing the number on your van. Why UK small businesses miss calls

Most of the calls a small firm misses come while someone is doing the paid work. The electrician is in a loft with no signal, the hairdresser is halfway through a cut, and the phone in the apron pocket buzzes on. A new caller often won't leave a voicemail and wait. They ring the next name in the search results, and by the time you call back they've booked.

A text within a minute gives them a reason to wait for you. It says you saw the call and asks what they need, and they can answer from the bus or the school gate. You reply between jobs, with their postcode and the problem in front of you.

We'd turn it on for any number new customers ring. The trade-off is that every text starts a conversation someone has to finish, so decide who reads the replies before you switch it on.

Which UK numbers can send a text back?

Mobile numbers. In Ofcom's numbering, 01 and 02 numbers are geographic, tied to an area code, 080 numbers are free to call, and mobile numbers start with 07 (Ofcom, 2026). UK mobile numbers send and receive texts. Most landline numbers can't, and in CallForges UK local and freephone numbers are for calls only.

Texts to UK phones from a number in another country aren't accepted, so they show as not delivered (Send SMS to contacts in other countries). If you serve UK customers from abroad, text them from a UK number. And a caller ringing from a landline, or with their number hidden, can't get a text at all, so keep voicemail on for them.

We'd keep the 01 or 02 number on the van and the website, since local customers know it, and add a 07 number just for the texts. The text then arrives from a number the caller didn't dial, so it has to say who you are.

What UK rules apply to a missed call text back?

PECR, the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, is the UK law on marketing by text, and the ICO enforces it. It applies to any organization sending marketing texts, and its rules are stricter for texts to individuals, a group that includes sole traders and some partnerships (ICO, updated 2025).

  • You can't send marketing texts to individuals without their specific consent, unless the soft opt-in applies: your own customers, about similar products or services, with a simple way to opt out when you took their number and in every text after (ICO guidance, PECR regulation 22).
  • Most of PECR covers unsolicited marketing. The ICO calls a message solicited when the person actively requested it, and even then you have to say who you are and give a contact address.
  • Service messages, sent for administrative or customer service purposes, aren't direct marketing. A service message with marketing in it can still count as direct marketing, even when that isn't its main purpose, and a neutral tone alone doesn't change that (ICO, Identify direct marketing, updated 2025).

PECR is the law; the ICO's pages explain how the regulator reads it. Your wording decides which side a text falls on. "Sorry we missed your call, what do you need?" is about the call they just made. Add "and 10% off boiler services this month" and you've put marketing in it. We'd keep offers out and end with "Reply STOP to opt out" anyway. Texting US customers too? Their rules differ: see Is missed call text back legal?

This is general information, not legal advice.

What should a UK missed call text say?

Put your business name first, because the text comes from a 07 number the caller has never seen, then one ask and one promise you'll keep. Keep it under 160 characters, the size of one text; an emoji cuts that to 70 and can split the message into several texts, each charged.

Three we'd start from, with your business name in the brackets:

  • "Hi, it's Dan at [Business]. Sorry I missed your call, I'm on a job till 3. Text me your postcode and what's wrong. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • "Thanks for calling [Business]. We're with clients. Reply with the day you'd like and we'll text you free times. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • "Thanks for calling [Business]. We open at 8am. Reply with your name and what you need and we'll ring you first thing. Reply STOP to opt out."

Send it within the first minute in the day, while the caller is still choosing who to ring. Out of hours, send the one that says when you'll call. If you often ring back within a couple of minutes, hold the text back a minute or two so nobody gets both.
