How auto dialers are billed: per seat, per minute or per line

Say you're pricing a dialer for five reps, and the three quotes that come back won't line up: one is per seat, one per minute, one per line that rings at once. Each charges for a different unit, so the cheapest one depends on how much your team dials.

Billing modelWhat you pay forWhat to ask
Per seatEach rep, by the month or the yearDoes the seat include minutes and a number, or do calls go on a separate bill?
Per minuteTalk time, at a rate for each countryHow is a minute rounded, and does a voicemail count?
Per lineEach extra line on a parallel dialerHow many lines come with the seat, and what does one more cost?
Flat "unlimited"One price, up to a fair use limitWhere does the limit sit, and what happens past it?
Setup feeA one-time charge for setup or trainingIs there one? Get the answer in writing.

Per minute suits a team that dials a few hours a week, since a quiet month costs less. Where the index found minutes billed on top of the seat, they ran about $0.02 a minute. A flat "unlimited" plan looks safe until a rep hits the fair use limit in the last week of the quarter.

For a team of five that dials every day, we'd take per seat with minutes included, so you know the bill before the month starts. A light dialer then costs as much as a heavy one, which is why we like a shared pool of minutes: the busy rep uses what the quiet one leaves.

What adds to the price of an auto dialer?

Minutes come first: the carrier charges for each connected minute, and the dialer company passes that on, inside the seat or on top of it.

Say a rep works a 150-dial call block and 40 calls reach a person or a voicemail. On CallForges that's at least 40 minutes, even if half were 10-second voicemails, and the 110 calls that rang out or got a busy signal cost nothing. Our Fair Usage Policy rounds any part of a minute up and counts each connected call, voicemail included, as at least 1 minute.

Numbers come next. Ask how many come with a seat and what an extra one costs, because a rep calling several regions may want more than one. Then ask which of the features you use today, say recording and CRM logging, cost extra.

Ask who pays to register your caller ID, too, since fewer people pick up a number labeled "Spam Likely". We include verified caller ID, where the carrier signs your calls to vouch for the number, and your company name as caller ID (CNAM) for US numbers on every plan.

Last, the contract. A minimum of ten seats doubles the bill for five reps, so ask about seat minimums and how you cancel. We have no seat minimum, and monthly plans cancel anytime. Our auto dialer software buyer's guide has the rest of the questions to bring to a demo.
