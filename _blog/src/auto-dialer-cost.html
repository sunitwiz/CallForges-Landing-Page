Minutes and the carrier's rates

Every call runs over the carrier's network, and the carrier charges for each minute. A dialer either includes a set number of minutes with each seat or charges you per minute, by country. Either way, ask how a minute is counted, because rounding and unanswered calls change the total.

On CallForges, calls are billed per minute and any part of a minute is rounded up. Each connected call, including one answered by voicemail, counts as at least 1 minute. Calls that don't connect (no answer, busy, invalid number) are not billed. Dialer calls use minutes the same way as manual calls. The Fair Usage Policy has the full rules.

A workspace's free minutes are one pool: the plan's minutes times the number of paid users. A rep who calls a lot can use minutes that a quieter rep doesn't. Calls to every other country are always billed per minute and never use the free minutes.

Registration fees

Ask which registrations your numbers need, and whether the dialer passes those fees on to you. On CallForges, registering your US numbers for verified caller ID and for your company name as caller ID (CNAM) is included on every plan. If you also text from US numbers, they register with the carriers first, and the carriers charge their own registration fees, paid from credits.

Is there a free auto dialer?

Not one that makes real calls without limits, because the carrier charges for every minute of a phone call and someone has to pay for it. A free dialer has to limit something: minutes, call length, features or time. Do power, parallel and predictive dialers cost different amounts?

They can, so ask. A dialer that rings several numbers at once may be priced per line or sold as a higher tier. On CallForges, the power dialer and the parallel dialer are both included on every plan, and you choose one per campaign.

CallForges doesn't offer a predictive dialer. Predictive dialers are built for large call centers with many agents, and they come with abandoned-call rules to manage. Power dialer vs predictive dialer explains the difference and which one fits a small sales team.
