What STIR/SHAKEN verification means for a phone number

It's the check at the far end of each call. The FCC's rules split STIR/SHAKEN into two halves: your phone company authenticates the caller ID when it sends the call, and the called person's network verifies it, which the rules define as confirming "that the caller identification information transmitted with a call it terminates was properly authenticated" (47 CFR 64.6300).

When people call a number "STIR/SHAKEN verified", they mean its calls are signed at A, the highest of the three levels in our STIR/SHAKEN guide. Your phone company signs A once it knows your business and your right to the number, and What is call attestation? explains how it decides.

The rules put the signing and checking on phone companies (47 CFR 64.6301), so registering with the carriers means giving your calling provider the facts it needs about you. Each provider must also take "affirmative, effective measures" against illegal calls, "including knowing its customers" (47 CFR 64.1200(n)(4)). That's why the form asks who you are and whom the carriers can contact.

These rules apply to phone companies. This is general information, not legal advice.

Pull out your business's registration documents first, because the carriers check what you type against official records. One business registration covers your whole workspace, and each extra registration asks for a little more:

Check two things before you submit. The postal code must belong to the state or region you pick, so a Sydney address needs a New South Wales code. And the extras are stricter than the business registration: the carriers check a caller ID name against US business records, and number reputation needs US details.

If you have an EIN or a DUNS number, we'd register with it. The business registration doesn't need one, but your caller ID name and number reputation do, and an approved registration can't be edited from the page.

Questions about STIR/SHAKEN verification

Do I need STIR/SHAKEN verification to make calls?

No. Calls from your CallForges US numbers to US phones work without it, on the free trial too. They're signed at level B from day one, because CallForges' own business is verified with the carrier, and registering takes them to A.

Can a business outside the US get its numbers verified?

Yes. The country on the first step isn't fixed: pick yours and enter your registration ID as it was issued, such as an Australian ACN or a UK company number. Once approved, verification starts on your US numbers. Your caller ID name and number reputation still need the US details in the table above.

Which phone numbers can be verified?

US local and mobile numbers, for calls to US phones. Toll-free numbers and numbers from other countries can't be, and Canada runs its own system, where only Canadian carriers sign Canadian numbers.

Does a verified number stop spam labels?

No. The called network weighs the signature along with how your number calls, so a number that makes hundreds of short calls a day can still be labeled at level A. Register number reputation too, and pace your dials.
