You bought 1,000 aged final expense leads at a fraction of the fresh-lead price, and by noon on day one you've dialed 90 and talked to six people. A parallel dialer looks like the answer: ring three at once, talk to whoever picks up. On aged insurance leads it can help, but those leads are consumers, and the rules on abandoned calls are written for exactly that. Keep the lines low, check your consent, and move anyone who answers onto a one-at-a-time dialer.

Key takeaways
  • Aged leads answer rarely, which is when a parallel dialer saves the most time.
  • They're consumers, so a second person who answers and hears silence can count as an abandoned call under federal rules.
  • Use two lines at most, check consent before you dial, and switch to a power dialer for callbacks and warm leads.

Why agents reach for a parallel dialer on aged leads

Aged leads are people who asked about insurance weeks or months ago and were sold to other agents first. Many have bought a policy, changed numbers or stopped answering unknown calls. On a list like that, most of your afternoon is ringing and voicemail greetings.

A parallel dialer rings several leads at once and connects you to the first person who answers. When few people pick up, the misses ring side by side instead of one after another, so you reach the same conversations sooner. Agents on insurance-forums.com ask which dialer to buy for exactly this kind of list (insurance-forums: dialer recommendation).

The catch: these are consumer calls

A parallel dialer can talk to only one person at a time. When two leads answer together, the second one is hung up, and that person heard nothing. On a business list that's an annoyance. On a consumer list, it's what the abandoned-call rules are about.

Under the FCC's rule, a telemarketing call is abandoned if the person isn't connected to a live sales representative within two seconds of finishing their greeting, and sellers may abandon no more than three percent of answered calls per campaign over each 30-day period. When no one is free, a recorded identification and opt-out message must play (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(7)). That's a law, and it applies to telemarketing calls to consumers.

Calls selling a policy are usually telemarketing. When a second person answers on a CallForges parallel campaign, the dialer hangs up with no message, so count each of those calls as one the rule is about, and keep them rare.

The other rules aged leads run into

An aged lead's age is the problem. The older the lead, the more likely the number changed hands, or the person registered on a do-not-call list after they filled in the form.

A safer way to work aged leads

Our take: if you use a parallel dialer on aged consumer leads at all, ring two lines, never more. Here's how we'd run the list:

  1. Scrub it against the national registry and your own do-not-call list, and drop numbers you can't show consent for.
  2. Run a first pass at two lines, in each lead's local calling hours, with a pause between rounds.
  3. Anyone who answered, asked for a callback or showed interest goes to a power dialer campaign, one call at a time.
  4. Watch your number. A burst of short, unanswered calls is what spam labeling looks for (why insurance agents' numbers get flagged).

The trade-off is speed. You'll get through the list more slowly than at four or five lines, and in exchange you keep your number clean and the collisions few. If a list is so cold that two lines still leave you waiting most of the hour, that's a sign the list is too old to be worth the risk, not a reason to add lines.
