Facts read on those pages:
- Scrub: "you must synchronize your lists with an updated version of the registry at least every 31 days."
- Fees: "Data for up to five area codes is free. Beginning October 1, 2026, the annual fee will be $85 per
  area code of data (after five) up to a maximum annual fee of $23,425."
- Established business relationship: 18 months after the last purchase or transaction; 3 months after an
  inquiry or application.
- Safe harbor: written procedures, training, monitoring, a company-specific do-not-call list, registry
  access no more than 31 days before calling, and the call was an error.
- The registry "is enforced by the FTC, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), and state officials";
  FCC rules also reach intrastate calls.
- Insurance and the TSR: the FTC guide says the FTC Act, and so the TSR, applies to insurance "to the extent
  that such business is not regulated by state law." The FCC's TCPA rules apply regardless.
- Not read from an official page (ecfr and fcc.gov blocked): the 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. calling window and the 3%
  abandoned-call limit in 64.1200. Verify on eCFR by hand before quoting.

**One-to-one consent rule: status as of 2026-11-25 (official sources)**
- Adopted in the FCC's Second Text Blocking Report and Order (2023). On Jan 24, 2025 the FCC postponed its
  effective date (DA 25-90). The same day the 11th Circuit vacated it in *Insurance Marketing Coalition Ltd.
  v. FCC*, 127 F.4th 303 (11th Cir. 2025); the court's mandate issued April 30, 2025.
- FCC Order DA 25-621 (released July 14, 2025) "repeals the revised version of section 64.1200(f)(9)" and
  "reinstates in our codified rules the version of section 64.1200(f)(9)" that applied before.
  https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-621A1.txt
- Published as a final rule at 90 FR 42137 (Aug 29, 2025, FR Doc. 2025-16641), effective August 29, 2025.
  The restored (f)(9) defines prior express written consent as a signed written agreement that "clearly
  authorizes the seller" to deliver autodialed or prerecorded telemarketing calls to the number given, with
  the two disclosures (it authorizes such calls; signing is not a condition of purchase). It has no
  one-seller-per-consent or "logically and topically related" text.
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-08-29/html/2025-16641.htm
- So the one-to-one consent rule is **not in force**. No later FCC action reviving it was found.
- Related, still pending: the "revoke all" part of 64.1200(a)(10) (one opt-out stops all later robocalls and
  robotexts from that caller on unrelated topics) was delayed by DA 26-12 (Jan 6, 2026): "the effective date
  of section 64.1200(a)(10) is extended to January 31, 2027." The rest of the revocation rules are not delayed.
  https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-12A1.txt
