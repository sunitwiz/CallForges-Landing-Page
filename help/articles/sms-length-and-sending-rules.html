SMS length and sending rules

What fits in one SMS, how a longer message is sent, and the checks every SMS passes before it goes.

Written by CallForges Support · Updated 14 November 2026

In this article
  1. Characters per SMS
  2. Long messages in parts
  3. The counter under the message box
  4. Quiet hours: SMS at night
  5. What is checked before an SMS goes

Characters per SMS

  • One SMS holds 160 characters.
  • With an emoji, or letters outside the standard SMS alphabet (such as Chinese, Arabic, Hindi or some accented letters), it holds 70.

Long messages in parts

  • A longer message goes out in parts that the contact's phone joins into one: 153 characters a part, or 67 with an emoji or another alphabet.
  • Each part is charged as one SMS.
  • A message can be up to 1,600 characters and 10 parts, your signature included. A longer one reads Too long: a text can be up to 10 parts.

The counter under the message box

As you write, the counter under the message box shows how many SMS the message goes out as and the characters used, for example 2 texts · 161 / 306: two SMS, 161 characters of the 306 that two parts hold.

Quiet hours: SMS at night

An SMS goes out when you send it, or at the time you scheduled it, whatever the hour where the contact is. Some countries limit marketing SMS to certain hours or days, such as Italy, Brazil and South Africa, so check the rules of your contacts' country and schedule SMS for their daytime.

What is checked before an SMS goes

  • The number can send SMS, and you may send from it.
  • The contact hasn't replied STOP. See SMS opt-outs.
  • You have credits. With 0 credits, an SMS is refused until you add credits.
  • A contact in the US is sent SMS only from a US number.

A scheduled SMS is checked again at its time. An SMS refused by one of these checks never leaves, so it costs nothing. An SMS the carriers fail to deliver after it leaves can carry a small fee for the failed message, charged from your credits about two days later.
